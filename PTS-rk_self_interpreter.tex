title: Self-interpretation in PTS-rk: a rank obstruction and an indexed approach
date: October 21, 2026

:::Abstract
The full original annotated PTS-rk admits a dependent indexed PHOAS
self-interpreter. The Rocq construction gives a finite numerical allocation
plan for every original typing derivation in a well-formed context, normal represented type and term
programs, and a normal originally typed interpreter whose application reduces
to the literal unreduced source term. The represented type is an ordinary
object-language argument, not an external source-type index. A faithful raw
HOAS tag preserves annotated syntax independently of conversion repairs;
equality or beta conversion of normal quoted programs implies literal
source equality at a fixed source scope, even across different signatures and derivations.
Open terms are covered by literal dependent closure and reopening.

The construction combines a finite rank-indexed Tarski interface with
independently enumerated and checked equality-only signature layers.
Every repair has independently typed endpoints and an actual normal
identity realizer. Structural quotation follows all six original typing
rules, including dependent application and conversion, without recursively
quoting synthesized validity derivations. The interpreter runs the represented
PHOAS program; it does not project a precomputed semantic source value.
No PTS rule, primitive, eta conversion or cumulativity is added.

The indices include a finite plan of telescope-length, syntax-node and literal
bounds as well as ranks. Coverage by a rank-only family and a practical
quotation-cost bound are not established. Fixed-rank elementary normalization
and non-uniform elementary representability motivated this investigation,
but the construction does not assume the manuscript-level elementary compiler.
Restricted rank, literal-generation and shared-index obstructions remain
valid; they do not contradict this sufficiently indexed positive result.
:::

# Why the complexity bounds suggest an indexed interpreter

There is positive computational evidence for a non-uniform self-interpreter.
The checked normalization development covers the **full original annotated
PTS**, not just a simply typed sublanguage. For a well-typed $d$-bounded
term $t$ in a well-formed context, $d$ bottom-up greedy passes produce its
normal form at the same original type. Writing $N$ for its node count and
$\operatorname{tower}_0(x)=x$,
$\operatorname{tower}_{h+1}(x)=2^{\operatorname{tower}_h(x)}$, the proved
operation-cost bound is

$$
\operatorname{cost}(\mathsf{normalize}_d(t))
 \leq \operatorname{tower}_{2d+1}(N+5d+2).
$$

Thus normalization is elementary for each fixed external $d$. Moreover,
every **closed, originally well-typed** term has the computable sufficient
bound

$$
d(t)=1+\mathsf{maxRankLiteral}(t)+\mathsf{nodes}(t).
$$

For the documented encoding size $s$, this bound is at most $2s+1$ and
its traversal costs at most $7s$. These are the public Rocq results
`fixed_rank_normalization` and `rank_bound_in_linear_time`.
The uniform automatic normalizer has an input-dependent tower-height bound;
this does not provide a uniform fixed-height elementary bound on all inputs.
The numerical rank bound here is a bound on redex heads, not merely the
rank of the final result type.
The open-context extension below computes a sufficient bound from both the
term and its context declarations; it requires no normalization-success oracle.

The manuscript's computational-model section, theorem label
`non-uniform-rep`, supplies the other half of the proposed argument:
for each elementary function $f$ there is a rank cost $c_f$ and, at each
base rank $j$, a numeral realizer

$$
F_j:\mathcal N_{j+c_f}\to\cdots\to\mathcal N_{j+c_f}\to\mathcal N_j,
\qquad
\mathcal N_j=\Pi X:\Ur j.(X\to X)\to X\to X,
$$

computing $f$ on canonical Church numerals. The rank cost is computed
from an explicit elementary program, using numeral-preserving rank
lowerings and composition. It is not a rank inferred from arbitrary
extensional black-box knowledge of $f$. The cited
[elementary substitution-basis theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23787)
gives the relevant arithmetic basis. A constructive compilation of the
fixed-$d$ normalization algorithm would therefore yield an effective
external rank budget depending on $d$, its elementary program and the
chosen serialization; no numerical formula for that compiler budget is
currently kernel-checked here.

:::Proposition[The complexity-to-self-reduction argument]
Assume the manuscript's non-uniform elementary representability theorem
and elementary encoding/decoding of annotated syntax as numbers. For each
fixed $d$ and base rank $j$, there is an original PTS numeral program
$S_{d,j}:\mathcal N_{j+c_d}\to\mathcal N_j$ which, on the numerical
code of any originally typed $d$-bounded term, returns the numerical code
of its full beta-normal form.
:::

:::Proof
On arbitrary numerical input, parse a raw annotated syntax tree, returning
a fixed default for malformed input. Perform $d$ greedy passes and encode
the result. For fixed $d$, the structural operations, serialization and
fixed-height bounds give an elementary total function; no type-checker
or unbounded search is needed in this algorithm. Apply non-uniform
representability to that function. On the independently specified typed,
$d$-bounded domain, the checked normalization theorem supplies correctness.
The program is fixed for $d,j$, not chosen anew for each input term.
:::

This is a genuine route to an **indexed numeric self-reducer**. Its
non-uniformity avoids the fixed-head rank obstruction.
However, a number coding a normal form is not an inhabitant of the
represented dependent type. A HOAS/PHOAS interpreter still needs actual
typed adapters and reification, including context and conversion coherence.
Further, normalization yields $\mathsf{nf}(t)=_\beta t$, not reduction
back to the original unreduced $t$. These distinctions determine what the
complexity argument proves and what remains to construct.

**Verification boundary.** Normalization and its stated operation costs
are checked. The generic elementary-function compiler is not formalized;
`latex/ROCQ_REFERENCES.md` explicitly records this boundary. The displayed
modulus construction in the manuscript also needs the local callback
correction $r\,f\,x$ in place of $r\,X\,f\,x$, because that record field
is already specialized to $X$. We do not silently treat the uncorrected
display as a checked inhabitant. The numerical proposition above is a
mathematical conditional, not a new Rocq axiom or a completed internal
HOAS interpreter.

# Question and source

The reference is Matt Brown and Jens Palsberg, *Breaking through the
Normalization Barrier: A Self-Interpreter for F-omega*, POPL 2016,
pp. 5--17, [doi:10.1145/2837614.2837623](https://doi.org/10.1145/2837614.2837623).
The supplied PDF has filename `2914770.2837623.pdf`;
the theorem labels below refer to its text, not to PDF page indices.

Their target is an **unquoter**: an external quotation operation assigns
a typed, beta-normal representation $\quoteof t$ to each term $t$, and
an object-language program recovers $t$ from this representation.
The strong version requires reduction to the original term,
$\mathsf{unquote}\,\quoteof t\bto t$, rather than merely conversion
to it. Quotation must preserve syntax injectively, with the paper's
precise treatment of equivalent type annotations. Returning the quotation
of a normal form is a different operation, called a self-reducer.

Section 3.3, Theorems 3.4--3.5, gives the shallow construction. A neutral
variable of type $\forall A:* . A\to A$ blocks each application;
substituting the polymorphic identity unblocks it. Sections 4--7 replace
this with a deeper representation using typed higher-order abstract
syntax, extensional instantiation functions, and interpretations of
types. Theorems 6.1--6.3 establish typing, normality, and injectivity up
to equivalence of type annotations; Theorem 7.4 establishes strong
recovery. Section 10 leaves dependent self-interpretation and
self-representation without impredicativity as open directions.

These are not impossibility results from strong normalization alone.
The paper specifically explains why a numerical diagonalization argument
does not automatically apply to typed quotation. The obstruction below
instead uses the exact PTS-rk product rule.

# The original calculus and the rank invariant

All claims use the existing `PTSrk.Syntax.Term nat n`, including lambda
annotations, `Typing.typ`, `Ranks.RankSorts`, and original beta reduction.
There is no cumulativity, added primitive, eta conversion, or replacement
typing judgment. Its sort and product rules are

$$
\Gamma\vdash\Ur i:\Ur 0,
\qquad
\frac{\Gamma\vdash A:\Ur a\quad
      \Gamma,x:A\vdash B:\Ur b}
     {\Gamma\vdash\Pi x:A.B:\Ur{\max(a+1,b)}}.
$$

The **type rank** of a typed term $t$ is $r$ when
$\Gamma\vdash t:T$ and $\Gamma\vdash T:\Ur r$.
This is the rank of its type, not its largest sort literal or a bound on
all redexes inside $t$. In well-formed contexts it exists, is unique,
and is invariant under beta conversion of typed terms. These facts are
already proved in `NormalizationComplexity/RedexRanks.v`.

:::Lemma[Application rank]
If $\Gamma$ is well formed and $f\,u$ is typed, then
$\rk_\Gamma(f\,u)\leq\rk_\Gamma(f)$.
:::

:::Proof
Invert the typing of the application to obtain
$f:\Pi x:A.B$ and $u:A$. If $A:\Ur a$ and
$B:\Ur b$ under the binder, the type of $f$ has rank
$\max(a+1,b)$. Substitution preserves $B:\Ur b$, so
$B[u/x]$ has rank $b$. Uniqueness of type rank removes any dependence
on the chosen derivation or intervening conversion rules.
:::

Consequently, for any well-typed finite application spine,

$$
\rk(E\,a_1\cdots a_m)\leq\rk(E).
$$

Arguments may be types, representations, or other terms. Their number and
their construction may depend arbitrarily on the represented program.
This bound already holds without a computable quotation operation.

:::Theorem[No universal fixed-head evaluator]
There is no closed typed PTS-rk term $E$ such that every closed typed
term $t$ is beta-convertible to a well-typed finite application spine
headed by $E$.
:::

:::Proof
Define closed types and inhabitants by

$$
 A_0=\Ur0,\quad A_{r+1}=\Pi x:A_r.\Ur0,
 \qquad
 v_0=\Ur0,\quad v_{r+1}=\lambda x:A_r.\Ur0.
$$

The original rules derive $A_r:\Ur r$ and $v_r:A_r$ for every
$r$. Suppose $E$ has type rank $R$. An application spine of $E$
has rank at most $R$, so invariance under conversion prevents it from
being convertible to $v_{R+1}$. This contradicts universality.
:::

The Rocq declaration is `RankObstruction.no_universal_fixed_head_evaluator`.
Its specification allows arbitrary externally chosen arguments and
arbitrary intermediate result types. It assumes neither an encoding nor
normality nor injectivity, making the exclusion stronger than failure of
one particular encoding.

This excludes a direct port of the actual fixed unquote programs in
Sections 3.3 and 7, including implicit type applications. A fixed wrapper
chosen once is another fixed $E$ and is excluded as well. Section 3.2
also permits implicit type *abstractions*. If interpreted as arbitrarily
many term-dependent outer wrappers, these lie outside the application-spine
theorem: external abstraction can raise rank. We do not claim to have
excluded every liberal interpretation of that allowance, externally
generated interpreter families, or a weaker observational semantics.

# What the sort cycle does and does not provide

Every $\Ur r$ has type $\Ur0$, but this does not place every type in
$\Ur0$. For example $A\to A$ has rank $r+1$ when $A:\Ur r$.
Thus there is no single sort playing the role of the arrow-closed
kind $*$ of System $F_\omega$. Replacing every occurrence of $*$ by
$\Ur0$ in the paper's code does not produce a typing derivation.

There is, however, an identity at every external rank:

$$
\Id_r=\Pi A:\Ur r.\,A\to A,
\qquad
\id_r=\lambda A:\Ur r.\lambda x:A.x,
\qquad
\Id_r:\Ur{r+1},\quad\id_r:\Id_r.
$$

The numerical index $r$ is part of generated syntax. Rocq quantification
over $r:\mathbb N$ produces a family of original PTS terms; it is not
an internal PTS quantifier over ranks. In particular an object numeral
cannot simply be substituted for the sort label in $\Ur r$.

For a blocker rank $r$ and an output-type rank $s$, define

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Code_r(A)&=\Id_r\to A,\\
\Dec_{r,s}&=\lambda A:\Ur s.\lambda q:\Code_r(A).\,q\,\id_r,\\
T_{r,s}&=\Pi A:\Ur s.\,\Code_r(A)\to A.
\end{aligned}
$$

The exact original judgments, proved in `RankedDecoder.v`, are

$$
\begin{aligned}
A:\Ur s&\ \Longrightarrow\ \Code_r(A):\Ur{\max(r+2,s)},\\
\Dec_{r,s}&:T_{r,s},\qquad
T_{r,s}:\Ur{\max(r+3,s+1)}.
\end{aligned}
$$

For any typed code $q:\Code_r(A)$,
$\Dec_{r,s}\,A\,q\bto q\,\id_r$. The raw packet
$p_r(t)=\lambda i:\Id_r.t$ (with the necessary de Bruijn shift)
satisfies $\Dec_{r,s}\,A\,p_r(t)\bto t$ in three beta steps.
This is a typed retraction for every original typed term, including
dependent terms. It is **not a quotation theorem**: $p_r(t)$ retains
every source redex. The declaration `typed_ranked_retraction` deliberately
makes only the retraction claim.

The theorem `decoder_type_not_in_result_universe` formalizes a failure of
self-specialization. The decoder cannot be instantiated at its own type
in its output universe: $T_{r,s}$ has rank at least $s+1$, hence cannot
also inhabit $\Ur s$. Increasing an
external index is therefore a real change of program, not the paper's
self-application of one polymorphic interpreter.

# HOAS replacement for the complete annotated syntax

Terms and types share the original syntax. The former first-order Church
AST is now replaced by a higher-order encoding: source variables become
target variables, and source binding becomes actual target lambda binding.
Original product domains and lambda annotations remain explicit data.
This represents every raw annotated source tree, not only well-typed ones.

Fix a carrier rank $r$ and an external source scope size $n$. Write
$X^n\to X$ for $n$ curried arguments of type $X$, with $X^0\to X=X$, and put

$$
\begin{aligned}
H(X)&=X\to(X\to X)\to X,\\
\mathsf{HOAS}_{r,n}
 &=\Pi X:\Ur r.\,
 X\to(X\to X)\to(X\to X)\\
 &\hspace{8mm}\to H(X)\to H(X)\to(X\to X\to X)\to(X^n\to X).
\end{aligned}
$$

The six handlers are numeral zero $z$, numeral successor $s$, sort $u$,
product $p$, lambda $l$, and application $a$. There is no variable
constructor. For an environment $\rho$ assigning carrier terms to source
variables, the executable fold is

$$
\begin{aligned}
F_\rho(\Ur k)&=u\,(s^kz),&
F_\rho(x)&=\rho(x),\\
F_\rho(\Pi x:A.B)&=p\,(F_\rho(A))\,
  (\lambda x:X.F_{\rho,x}(B)),\\
F_\rho(\lambda x:A.b)&=l\,(F_\rho(A))\,
  (\lambda x:X.F_{\rho,x}(b)),\\
F_\rho(f\,t)&=a\,(F_\rho(f))\,(F_\rho(t)).
\end{aligned}
$$

The binder clauses shift every handler and outer environment entry and
map the newly bound source variable to the new target variable. This is
HOAS inside the original PTS syntax, not a Rocq function hidden in an
abstract syntax constructor. The closed quote $C_{r,n}(t)$ abstracts over
$X,z,s,u,p,l,a$, then over the $n$ carrier variables needed to close its
source environment. For example, at source scope zero,

$$
\begin{aligned}
C_{r,0}(\lambda x:\Ur k.x)
={}&\lambda X:\Ur r.\lambda z:X.\lambda s:X\to X.\\
 &\lambda u:X\to X.\lambda p:H(X).\lambda l:H(X).\\
 &\lambda a:X\to X\to X.\ l\,(u\,(s^kz))\,(\lambda x:X.x).
\end{aligned}
$$

:::Proposition[Normal, faithfully encoded raw HOAS]
For every original $t:\mathsf{Term}\ \mathbb N\ n$ and every external $r$,
the original rules derive
$\vdash C_{r,n}(t):\mathsf{HOAS}_{r,n}$ and
$\vdash\mathsf{HOAS}_{r,n}:\Ur{r+3}$. The quotation is fully beta-normal.
At each fixed source scope $n$,

$$
C_{r,n}(t)\bconv C_{s,n}(t')
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad r=s\ \text{and}\ t=t'
$$

as literal annotated source syntax.
:::

The binder handler $H(X)$ has rank $r+2$, so accepting this handler raises
the code type to rank $r+3$. This is one higher than the replaced
first-order representation. Additional carrier-variable arguments do not
raise that bound. The existence endpoint in `ChurchSyntax` is
`every_original_term_has_church_code`. The fidelity theorem is
`ChurchSyntaxFaithful.quote_conversion_iff`. Normality includes the
generated lambda annotations. Injectivity is proved by structural
inversion under an injective variable environment, not inferred from
evaluation correctness. It therefore retains distinctions between a
reducible source annotation and its contractum.

Applying a quotation to its carrier and six handlers performs seven
administrative beta steps:

$$
C_{r,n}(t)\,X\,z\,s\,u\,p\,l\,a
 \bto \lambda x_1:X.\cdots\lambda x_n:X.F_{\vec x}(t).
$$

`ChurchSyntaxFold.run_fold` establishes this reduction, and `run_typing`
derives the original typing of the actual application with result type
$X^n\to X$. No subject expansion is used. With $n=0$ the result is
the unwrapped fold. Source sort labels remain numeral data; no lower
bound on $r$ is imposed by their magnitude.

**Terminological boundary.** This is a PTS-typed encoding of raw syntax.
Its carrier does not depend on the source term's type. In the terminology
of the paper it is an *untyped source representation*, despite its actual
target typing theorem. It is not the source-type-indexed construction
discussed next. No claim is made that every inhabitant of its code type
arises from source syntax, or that its arbitrary algebras perform typed
unquotation. Instantiating its carrier by another code type requires
$r=s+3$, without cumulativity or an internal rank quantifier.

# Which indices a raw-HOAS decoder must retain

There are three different quantities: a redex bound $d$, the exact rank $s$
of the result type, and the largest literal sort label $\ell$ in the source.
The conservative bound $d(t)$ in the introduction includes literal labels.
An arbitrary sufficient redex bound need not do so: every $\Ur k$ is
already normal, has redex bound zero, and has type $\Ur0$, of rank zero,
regardless of $k$.

The raw HOAS quote deliberately turns these labels into numeral data.
In fact, `LiteralObstruction.quote_literal_exact` proves

$$
\mathsf{maxRankLiteral}(C_{r,n}(t))=r.
$$

:::Theorem[Literal-generation obstruction]
For any fixed closed raw term $E$ and carrier index $r$, if
$E\,C_{r,0}(\Ur k)\bconv\Ur k$, then

$$
k\leq\max\{\mathsf{maxRankLiteral}(E),r\}.
$$

Consequently no such $E$ unquotes all normal sorts from these codes,
even when the source redex bound and result-type rank are both zero.
:::

:::Proof
Original beta reduction never creates a larger literal sort label.
Conversion to the normal sort $\Ur k$ implies reduction to it by the
common-reduct theorem. The applied code contains only the literal labels
of $E$ and the single carrier label $r$. Taking $k$ one greater than their
maximum gives a counterexample. No typing premise on $E$ is needed.
:::

The theorem `no_fixed_type_argument_unquoter` also permits the ordinary
type argument $\Ur0$ before the raw code. This argument supplies none of
the missing $k$. Thus indexing an interpreter by **only** redex bound and
result-type rank does not solve raw-code unquotation. A family whose
generated syntax additionally depends on a literal ceiling $\ell$, or on
the conservative bound which includes it, is not excluded. Neither is a
representation carrying additional literal-bearing semantic data. The
obstruction does not concern numeric code-to-code self-reduction: an output
code can represent $k$ by data without generating the literal $\Ur k$.

# Full-PTS normalization connected to HOAS

The executable metalevel construction in `RankedNormalization` is

$$
\mathsf{NQ}_{r,d}(t)=C_{r,n}(\mathsf{rounds}_d(t)).
$$

For every original typed, $d$-bounded term in a well-formed context,
the source of this quotation is its full normal form, reachable by
original reduction and retaining its original type. The resulting closed
HOAS code is normal and has type $\mathsf{HOAS}_{r,n}$. For two such
sources in the same context, possibly with different sufficient budgets
and types, the checked theorem gives

$$
\mathsf{NQ}_{r,d}(t)\bconv\mathsf{NQ}_{r',e}(u)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
r=r'\ \text{and}\ t\bconv u.
$$

The automatic variant uses $d(t)$ and covers every closed original typed
term without a supplied bound or normalization-success hypothesis.
It identifies beta-convertible sources, unlike the raw quotation, which
retains exact syntax. In particular, canonicalization is not being mistaken
for syntax-level injectivity.

The normalization stage inherits the operation cost from the introduction.
The additional checked output-size bound, for
$L=\mathsf{maxRankLiteral}(t)$ and $N=\mathsf{nodes}(t)$, is

$$
\mathsf{nodes}(\mathsf{NQ}_{r,d}(t))
 \leq 34+2n+(2L+5)\operatorname{tower}_{d}(N).
$$

The factor involving $L$ accounts for unary label data in this HOAS
representation. This is an output-size theorem, not a charged runtime for
HOAS construction or numeric serialization. The canonicalizer is an
executable Rocq function on the original syntax; it is not yet a PTS term
implementing the conditional numerical program from the introduction.

## Open contexts, canonical types and hereditary substitution

The canonicalizer now covers **every original well-typed open term**.
Let $L(\Gamma)$ and $S(\Gamma)$ be the maximum literal label and maximum
node count among the declarations of $\Gamma$, with empty maxima zero.
Define executable metalevel functions

$$
\begin{aligned}
d(\Gamma,t)
 &=1+\max\{L(\Gamma),\mathsf{maxRankLiteral}(t)\}
       +\max\{S(\Gamma),\mathsf{nodes}(t)\},\\
N_\Gamma(t)&=\mathsf{rounds}_{d(\Gamma,t)}(t).
\end{aligned}
$$

`OpenCanonical.budget_sound` proves this bound sufficient from original
well-formedness and typing. `nf_correct` proves full normality, reduction
from $t$, original type preservation and success of the bounded normalizer.
The function consumes syntax, not an erased typing proof or a supplied
normalization certificate. Its correctness requires original well-typedness;
arbitrary raw input need not be normalized by these finite rounds.
The normalization-stage cost is the earlier tower bound at $d(\Gamma,t)$;
no new runtime bound for context scanning or code construction is claimed.

For originally typed terms, possibly in different well-formed contexts of
the same scope,

$$
N_\Gamma(t)=N_\Delta(u)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad t\bconv u.
$$

This compares original scoped syntax, not denotations of variables in
unrelated environments. In particular, changing a valid typing context or
sufficient normalization budget cannot change a term's normal form.

Let $\sigma:\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta$ be an **actual original typed
substitution**: $\Delta\vdash\sigma(i):\sigma(\Gamma!!i)$ for each $i$.
Set

$$
\overline\sigma(i)=N_\Delta(\sigma(i)),
\qquad H_{\Delta,\sigma}(t)=N_\Delta(t[\sigma]).
$$

:::Theorem[Full-PTS canonical substitution]
For well-formed original contexts, $\Gamma\vdash t:T$ and original
typed $\sigma:\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta$, the normalized substitution
$\overline\sigma$ is again originally typed, and

$$
\begin{aligned}
N_\Delta(t[\sigma])
 &=N_\Delta\bigl(N_\Gamma(t)[\overline\sigma]\bigr),\\
\Delta&\vdash H_{\Delta,\sigma}(t):H_{\Delta,\sigma}(T).
\end{aligned}
$$

For original typed $\tau:\Delta\Rightarrow\Theta$, composition gives

$$
H_{\Theta,\tau}\bigl(H_{\Delta,\sigma}(t)\bigr)
 =H_{\Theta,\rho}(t),
\qquad \rho(i)=H_{\Theta,\tau}(\sigma(i)).
$$

Identity substitution returns $N_\Gamma(t)$.
:::

:::Proof
Reduction commutes with simultaneous substitution in both the term and its
entries. Normalizing an entry also reduces its dependent lookup type;
original validity, preservation and conversion establish `TypedSub` for
the normalized entries. The substituted terms are originally typed and
convertible, so normal-form uniqueness yields literal equality. Validity
supplies the exact sort needed to normalize the result type too, without
cumulativity. Composition follows from ordinary substitution composition
and these normalization equations.
:::

`CanonicalSubstitution` proves these statements for the **full original
calculus**, including dependent products, sorts, conversion and annotations.
The outer normalization cannot be dropped. A checked regression uses

$$
\Gamma=(f:\Pi X:\Ur0.\Ur0),\quad A=f\,\Ur0,
\quad\sigma(f)=\lambda X:\Ur0.X.
$$

The source type and substitution entry are both normal and originally
typed, but $A[\sigma]=(\lambda X:\Ur0.X)\,\Ur0$ is a redex with normal
form $\Ur0$. Normalizing entries alone does not make ordinary substitution
canonical.

Contexts can also be normalized without changing the term being typed:

$$
\mathsf{NC}(\varnothing)=\varnothing,
\qquad
\mathsf{NC}(\Gamma,A)=\mathsf{NC}(\Gamma),N_\Gamma(A).
$$

`CanonicalContexts` constructs original well-formedness and actual identity
substitutions in **both directions**. For well-formed $\Gamma$,

$$
\Gamma\vdash t:T
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathsf{NC}(\Gamma)\vdash t:T.
$$

The terms $t,T$, including annotations, are literally unchanged.
The declarations become normal; context normalization is idempotent and
reachable by original context reduction. Computing $N(t)$ in either context
gives the same result. Formation ranks come from the original `Type`-valued
well-formedness witness, not elimination of an existential `Prop` proof.

We therefore obtain concrete **distinct** syntax and type-index maps:

$$
C_{r,n}(t),\qquad J_{r,\Gamma}(A)=C_{r,n}(N_\Gamma(A)).
$$

`CanonicalTypeIndex` proves that the former retains literal annotated
syntax, whereas equality of the latter, on originally typed inputs at
fixed $r$, is exactly original beta conversion. Canonical indices satisfy
the hereditary substitution and composition equations after quotation.
On the convertible types $(\lambda X:\Ur0.X)\,\Ur0$ and $\Ur0$, faithful
codes differ while canonical indices agree; the faithful code of the first
is not even convertible to its own canonical index. These maps genuinely
avoid the shared-map obstruction.

Both maps produce closed inhabitants of the **homogeneous HOAS code type**;
$J(A)$ is not itself a source type at the universe of $A$. Hereditary
substitution here acts on original syntax before quotation, not by running
an object-language program on a code. This completes a full-PTS canonical
context/index algebra, but not an internal operation
$\mathsf{El}(J(A))\bconv A$ or a translation of every dependent family to
abstract represented arguments. These remain object-language obligations,
not consequences to assume from normalization. No general PHOAS impossibility
follows.

## A full dependent weak interpretation with faithful syntax

There is nevertheless an actual closed indexed PTS decoder for a weaker
representation. Normalize both the source and its type **during external
quotation**, and retain the original raw HOAS tag to preserve syntax.
Put $C_r=\mathsf{HOAS}_{r,0}$ and, for $A:\Ur s$, define

$$
\begin{aligned}
W_r(A)&=(A\to C_r\to A)\to A,\\
\mathsf{pack}_r(A,u,c)&=\lambda h:A\to C_r\to A.\,h\,u\,c,\\
\mathsf{select}_r(A)&=\lambda x:A.\lambda c:C_r.x,\\
\mathsf{WDec}_{r,s}&=\lambda A:\Ur s.\lambda q:W_r(A).\,
 q\,(\mathsf{select}_r(A)).
\end{aligned}
$$

Its exact original typings are

$$
\begin{aligned}
W_r(A)&:\Ur{\max(s+2,r+5)},\\
\mathsf{WDec}_{r,s}&:\Pi A:\Ur s.\,W_r(A)\to A,\\
(\Pi A:\Ur s.\,W_r(A)\to A)&:\Ur{\max(s+3,r+6)}.
\end{aligned}
$$

For a closed original judgment $t:A$, let
$\mathsf{nf}(v)=\mathsf{rounds}_{d(v)}(v)$ and define

$$
Q_r(t:A)=\mathsf{pack}_r
  (\mathsf{nf}(A),\mathsf{nf}(t),C_{r,0}(t)).
$$

:::Theorem[Full closed weak interpretation]
Every original closed derivation $\vdash t:A$ has a sort witness
$\vdash A:\Ur s$. The computed $Q_r(t:A)$ is fully normal, has original
type $W_r(\mathsf{nf}(A))$, and satisfies

$$
\mathsf{WDec}_{r,s}\,\mathsf{nf}(A)\,Q_r(t:A)
 \bto\mathsf{nf}(t)\bconv t.
$$

The applied decoder has original type $A$. Moreover, conversion between
two such normal packages, even with different supplied source types,
implies equality of their carrier indices and literal equality of their
annotated source terms.
:::

:::Proof
Original type validity supplies $s$; normalization and preservation give
$\mathsf{nf}(A):\Ur s$ and $\mathsf{nf}(t):A$. Original conversion then
types the payload at $\mathsf{nf}(A)$. Both payload and type are normal;
the raw HOAS tag is normal independently of source reducibility.
The package body is headed by its bound variable, so the whole quotation,
including its annotation, is normal. The decoder performs five
administrative beta steps to the payload. Its result can be converted
back to type $A$. Conversion between normal packages implies their literal
equality; extracting their tags and applying raw-HOAS fidelity recovers
the exact source syntax.
:::

`WeakFullInterpretation.full_closed_weak_interpretation` proves coverage
from original closed typing alone. `quote_conversion_injective` proves
the separate fidelity claim. The supplied source type or derivation is
not asserted injectively recoverable: only the annotated term is tagged.
The quote is a total external syntax computation; its normality theorem
has the original typing premise. The decoder's $s$ is an **exact** sort
rank, obtained propositionally by type validity, not a larger bound used
via nonexistent cumulativity.

This construction includes dependent products, sort terms, reducible
annotations and all other original closed typed syntax. It requires no
elementary-representability assumption. But its computation occurs in the
external quoter, and its decoder ignores the HOAS tag. It is therefore a
normal, faithful **weak semantic-payload interpretation**, not an internal
interpreter of pure HOAS and not Brown--Palsberg strong unquotation of the
unreduced source. The literal obstruction is avoided because the normal
payload can itself contain the source's literal labels. The computational
challenge left by the introduction is to move normalization and typed
reification into the object program instead of supplying this payload.

# Independent Brown--Palsberg PHOAS investigation

The paper's Figure 3 abstracts a type interpretation $F:*\to *$.
For the arrow fragment its pretranslation uses
$\widehat{A\to B}=F\widehat A\to F\widehat B$, while the representation
of a term of type $A$ has carrier $F\widehat A$. Its abstraction and
application handlers therefore involve $F(F A\to F B)$.
Unquotation specializes this same $F$ to the type identity.

:::Theorem[Obstruction to the literal single-family interface]
Let $F:\Pi A:\Ur a.\Ur b$. If $A,B:\Ur a$ and
$F(F A\to F B)$ has any original PTS type, then $a=b+1$.
If the same family type admits the identity
$\lambda A:\Ur a.A$, then $a=b$. Thus these requirements cannot hold
simultaneously, even when different input and output ranks are allowed.
:::

:::Proof
Application inversion and uniqueness of typing force the argument
$F A\to F B$ to inhabit $\Ur a$. Both $F A$ and $F B$ inhabit
$\Ur b$, so the original product rule places that arrow in $\Ur{b+1}$.
Uniqueness of sort ranks gives $a=b+1$; conversion cannot change it.
The literal identity already has type $\Pi A:\Ur a.\Ur a$.
Uniqueness of typing followed by product and sort conversion inversion
therefore gives $a=b$. The two equations contradict each other.
:::

`BrownPalsberg.no_single_family_identity_interface` proves this statement
from actual original typing judgments, including arbitrary alternative
types used in application derivations. `no_literal_interface` specializes
the arguments to the explicitly constructed closed type $A_a:\Ur a$,
so the obstruction is not a vacuous lack of source types.
This is independent of the fixed-head evaluator theorem. It excludes the
literal common-family interface, not every possible PHOAS encoding.

## A rank-indexed repair of the type interpretation

For the independent simply typed source, replace the one family by external
parameters $F_i:\Pi A:\Ur i.\Ur i$ and a ground carrier $o:\Ur0$. Define

$$
\begin{aligned}
\widehat o&=o,\\
\widehat{A\to B}
 &=F_a\widehat A\to F_b\widehat B,
 &a&=\operatorname{ord}(A),\quad b=\operatorname{ord}(B),\\
\mathcal I_F(A)
 &=F_{\operatorname{ord}(A)}\widehat A.
\end{aligned}
$$

Both $\widehat A$ and $\mathcal I_F(A)$ inhabit precisely
$\Ur{\operatorname{ord}(A)}$. The executable `pretype` and
`interpreted_type` have original typing proofs, strong recovery to the
ordinary source type code under $F_i=\lambda A:\Ur i.A$, and proved finite
dependence on indices at most $\operatorname{ord}(A)$.
These are syntactic functions generating PTS terms; the index $i$ is
external, not a new PTS sort variable.

With $c=\max(a+1,b)$, the corresponding polymorphic handlers are

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf{Abs}_{a,b}
 &=\Pi A:\Ur a.\Pi B:\Ur b.\,
  (F_a A\to F_b B)\to F_c(F_a A\to F_b B),\\
\mathsf{App}_{a,b}
 &=\Pi A:\Ur a.\Pi B:\Ur b.\,
  F_c(F_a A\to F_b B)\to F_a A\to F_b B.
\end{aligned}
$$

Both handler types inhabit $\Ur{c+1}$; `indexed_handler_type_sorts`
proves their formation. When the families are identities, the actual
original lambda terms

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf{abs}^{\mathrm{id}}_{a,b}
 &=\lambda A:\Ur a.\lambda B:\Ur b.\lambda f:A\to B.f,\\
\mathsf{app}^{\mathrm{id}}_{a,b}
 &=\lambda A:\Ur a.\lambda B:\Ur b.
   \lambda f:A\to B.\lambda x:A.f\,x
\end{aligned}
$$

inhabit these exact interpreted handler types by conversion.

`indexed_identity_handlers_typing` proves their original typing.
The two strong beta equations are `indexed_abs_recovers` and
`indexed_app_recovers`. Each is a closed original term at its external pair
$(a,b)$. This genuinely repairs the local single-family rank conflict:
the outer arrow uses $F_c$, not $F_a$ or a universal $F$.
It does not by itself supply a closed quotation package or a dependent
conversion theorem.

## Source-type-indexed HOAS term prequotation

The new `TypedHOAS` construction uses the independent computational source
derivations described below. Unlike the raw-AST representation, its result
type records the source type. Write $I(A)=\mathcal I_F(A)$ and suppose
specialized operations are supplied with types

$$
\mathsf{abs}_{A,B}:(I(A)\to I(B))\to I(A\to B),
\qquad
\mathsf{app}_{A,B}:I(A\to B)\to I(A)\to I(B).
$$

The executable derivation-directed prequotation is

$$
\begin{aligned}
q(x)&=\rho(x),\\
q(\lambda x:|A|.t)&=\mathsf{abs}_{A,B}
  (\lambda x:I(A).q(t)),\\
q(f\,u)&=\mathsf{app}_{A,B}\,q(f)\,q(u),
\end{aligned}
$$

where the source derivation supplies $A,B$ and all binder lifts are explicit
in the original scoped syntax. The theorem `prequote_typed` derives
$q(t):I(C)$ from independent source evidence $t:C$, a correctly interpreted
source-variable environment, and the stated family and operation typings.
The premises type the algebra, not its recursively constructed output.

The bounded variant uses the computable budget
$K(D)=\max(\operatorname{ord}(C),b(D))$ for a derivation $D:t:C$, where
$b(D)$ is the application budget defined below. The theorem
`prequote_bounded_typed` requires family laws only at $i\leq K(D)$ and
case laws only when $\operatorname{ord}(A\to B)\leq K(D)$.
Including the result order matters: a high-order identity lambda can have
application budget zero. Bounded case laws still quantify over source
types, not merely a finite list of monomorphic variables. The finite
implementation below specializes the rank-pair-polymorphic handlers above.

Specializing the families to the type identities and the operations to
the actual closed Brown--Palsberg handlers gives strong recovery of the
original annotated term (`identity_interpretation_recovers`). This is
also related to actual replacement of already generated syntax:
`prequote_substitution` proves that simultaneous substitution commutes
with prequotation, provided it sends the embedded source variables to
the stated new variables. The theorem `substitution_identity_recovers`
then reduces that substituted prequote to the source under the new
renaming, assuming the substitution actually supplies the specified
identity families and handlers. It does not assume that the quotation
itself recovers, nor assert existence of the missing finite substitution
telescope. Annotation recovery is part of the original reduction proof.

`TypedHOASAlgebra` proves original typing of the actual two-application
specializations of the closed identity handlers. Its theorem
`typed_identity_interpretation` combines original typing at the ordinary
source type with strong recovery, for every independent source derivation
in an originally typed source environment. This concrete identity
interpretation is not asserted normal: normal prequotation and subsequent
identity interpretation are different phases.

In `TypedHOASFaithful`, neutral variable families and handlers give full
annotation normality (`prequote_normal`). A structural eraser separately
strips interpreted type applications and the two constructor patterns;
`erase_prequote` yields exactly $\operatorname{rename}(\rho,t)$.
Consequently `prequote_injective` recovers literal annotated source syntax
when $\rho$ is injective, even when the two derivations, result types and
handler palettes differ. `prequote_conversion_injective` strengthens this
to comparison by original beta conversion of the neutral prequotes.
This is not proof irrelevance for derivation evidence.

These earlier results concern parameterized prequotation. The generic
typing theorem and the variable-palette normality theorem cannot be combined
by silently assuming infinitely many differently typed variables in one
finite context. The following construction discharges that obligation with
an actual finite context and actual typed parameter substitutions.
Nor is every neutral interpretation family impossible in a finite context:
one $h:\Id_0$ already gives $F_r=h\,\Ur r$, because $\Ur r:\Ur0$.
The construction below uses separate variable families, not this alternative.

## Closing the finite rank-indexed PHOAS representation

For each external $k$, bind $o:\Ur0$, the $k+2$ families
$F_0,\ldots,F_{k+1}$, and two polymorphic handlers
$\mathsf{Abs}_{a,b},\mathsf{App}_{a,b}$ for each $0\leq a,b\leq k$.
The handler types are the indexed types displayed above. This deliberately
includes some unused pairs. Their arrow rank can be $k+1$, which explains
the extra family $F_{k+1}$. The finite parameter context $\Delta_k$ has

$$
1+(k+2)+2(k+1)^2
$$

binders. Its original well-formedness, all annotation normality, and the
actual ground, family and handler lookup typings are proved. It depends
on $k$, not on the number of source type shapes or the syntax of a
particular program. There are infinitely many simple types at fixed order;
the handlers cover them by ordinary PTS type application, not by adding
one variable for each type.

Specialize a handler at the two pretypes:

$$
\mathsf{abs}_{A,B}
 =\mathsf{Abs}_{a,b}\,\widehat A\,\widehat B,
\qquad
\mathsf{app}_{A,B}
 =\mathsf{App}_{a,b}\,\widehat A\,\widehat B,
\qquad a=\operatorname{ord}(A),\ b=\operatorname{ord}(B).
$$

These applied handlers remain neutral. `PHOASAlgebra` proves their actual
original typing and that of the resulting derivation-directed prequotation.
`PHOASFaithful` accounts for the two extra type arguments in a new eraser;
it proves full normality and literal annotated-source injectivity, also
when comparing quotes by beta conversion. No disjointness of handler and
family variable names is assumed by the generic eraser theorem.

For a term-closed independent source derivation $D:t:A$ whose sole free
parameter is $o$, and $K(D)\leq k$, put

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf{PCode}_{k,A}&=\Pi\Delta_k.\,\mathcal I_F(A),\\
\mathsf{PQuote}_k(D)&=\lambda\Delta_k.\,q_F(D).
\end{aligned}
$$

Here $\Pi\Delta_k$ and $\lambda\Delta_k$ are notation for finite lists of
**original** products and annotated lambdas; they are not added syntax.
`PHOASTelescope` implements their closure and actual sequential application.
Its application-typing theorem uses a typed substitution for the supplied
arguments, not subject expansion from a known reduct. `PHOASPackage`
constructs the concrete context and code. The computed quote is closed,
normal, and originally typed at $\mathsf{PCode}_{k,A}$. At fixed $k$,
conversion of quotes implies literal equality of their annotated sources,
even for different source derivations and result types.

`PHOASRecovery` proves that replacing the used families by their type
identities and the handlers by their actual closed identity interpretations
reduces the prequote to the original source. Its hypotheses concern only
the ranks and rank pairs within $K(D)$; no correctness property is required
of fallback entries outside the finite palette. This is a theorem about
actual replacement of generated syntax, not about separately generating a
second term with different parameters.

The parameter substitution is itself realized by an actual closed decoder.
Writing $\boldsymbol\iota_k$ for the finite list of type identities and
identity Abs/App handlers in the telescope's binder order, put

$$
\mathsf{PDec}_{k,A}
 =\lambda q:\mathsf{PCode}_{k,A}.\lambda o:\Ur0.
     q\,o\,\boldsymbol\iota_k.
$$

:::Theorem[Closed finite PHOAS unquotation]
For every independent term-closed Church-STLC derivation $D:t:A$ and
$k\geq K(D)$, the actual original closed terms satisfy

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf{PQuote}_k(D)&:\mathsf{PCode}_{k,A},\\
\mathsf{PDec}_{k,A}&:
 \mathsf{PCode}_{k,A}\to(\Pi o:\Ur0.|A|),\\
\mathsf{PDec}_{k,A}\,\mathsf{PQuote}_k(D)
 &\bto\lambda o:\Ur0.t.
\end{aligned}
$$

The quote is normal and injective on original annotated source syntax
at fixed $k$, even under comparison by target beta conversion.
:::

`PHOASDecoder.closed_phoas_interpretation` proves the typing and strong
recovery claims; the separate package theorem proves fidelity. The ground
abstraction is inside the decoder, not an external operation appended to
evaluation. All finite parameter-typing and substitution premises are
discharged by the constructed context and identity arguments. Code-type
formation is proved independently of source inhabitation. The current sort
theorems provide a universe witness, not a separately verified closed-form
rank formula.

This decoder is indexed by **both $k$ and the full simple type $A$**,
not by $k$ and $\operatorname{ord}(A)$ alone. At fixed $(k,A)$ it is the
same raw PTS program for every source derivation within budget, independent
of program size; it is not chosen per source term. Internalizing the type
interpretation as an additional argument, to remove the full-$A$ external
index, is not provided by this earlier decoder. The later dependent program
decoder supplies an ordinary represented-type argument instead.

The executable regression quotes
$\lambda x:o.(\lambda y:o.y)x$ with computed budget one. Its actual closed
PHOAS package computes to a normal term, while its decoder strongly
recovers the original still-reducible source, including the outer ground
abstraction. No normal form or semantic value is precomputed and stored
in this quotation. This completes the finite PHOAS construction for the
independent STLC source, not for all original dependent PTS syntax.

This is a Church-style, algebra-parametric typed HOAS construction inside
PTS-rk. The term PHOAS here emphasizes that the representation abstracts
its interpretation families and operations. It should not be confused with
adding a native Rocq inductive datatype and calling that an internal PTS
representation. In [Chlipala's PHOAS](https://adam.chlipala.net/papers/PhoasICFP08/),
the variable family is likewise an explicit parameter, but that work's
metatheoretic treatment is not a theorem about all inhabitants of these
PTS code types. Our proofs concern the actual quoted outputs; they assume
no parametricity axiom or no-junk theorem for arbitrary code inhabitants.

## Genuinely dependent indexed handlers

The local rank-indexed repair is not limited to constant codomains. Let
$F_i:\Ur i\to\Ur i$, $A:\Ur a$ and
$B:(F_a A)\to\Ur b$, and set $c=\max(a+1,b)$ and

$$
D_F(A,B)=\Pi x:F_a A.\,F_b(B\,x).
$$

The actual original product rule gives $D_F(A,B):\Ur c$. Consequently
the two dependent constructor interfaces are

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf{DAbs}_{a,b}
 &=\Pi A:\Ur a.\Pi B:(F_a A\to\Ur b).\,
     D_F(A,B)\to F_c(D_F(A,B)),\\
\mathsf{DApp}_{a,b}
 &=\Pi A:\Ur a.\Pi B:(F_a A\to\Ur b).\,
     F_c(D_F(A,B))\to D_F(A,B).
\end{aligned}
$$

Both inhabit exactly $\Ur{c+1}$. In particular, the codomain of an
applied constructor $h_{\mathrm{app}}:\mathsf{DApp}_{a,b}$ really depends
on the represented argument:

$$
h_{\mathrm{app}}\,A\,B\,q_f\,q_u
 : F_b(B\,q_u).
$$

At identity families, the closed original handlers

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf{dabs}_{a,b}
 &=\lambda A:\Ur a.\lambda B:A\to\Ur b.
   \lambda f:(\Pi x:A.Bx).f,\\
\mathsf{dapp}_{a,b}
 &=\lambda A:\Ur a.\lambda B:A\to\Ur b.
   \lambda f:(\Pi x:A.Bx).\lambda x:A.f\,x
\end{aligned}
$$

inhabit these exact interpreted interfaces by proved beta conversion.
Their applications reduce to $f$ and $f\,u$, respectively.
`DependentPHOAS` proves formation, operation typing, identity specialization
and these reduction equations. It also derives the original type $B\,u$
of the actual applied identity App handler directly, before reducing it.
No eta conversion is used.

These local handlers alone do not construct the supplied family
$B:(F_a A)\to\Ur b$ recursively from every
source dependent family $B_0:A\to\Ur b$. The domains differ while $F_a$
is abstract. Treating this input as already translated would assume away
the central coherence obligation.

For the candidate convention that interprets sort literals by themselves,
a source type $T:\Ur b$, regarded as a source term, has representation
carrier $F_0(\Ur b)$. Using that representation as an annotation requires
a separate operation

$$
\mathsf{El}_b:F_0(\Ur b)\to\Ur b.
$$

Its type inhabits $\Ur1$, and the type identity at rank $b$ is an actual
inhabitant at identity families. Both facts and its beta equation are
checked. Merely binding an abstract $\mathsf{El}_b$ does not give it the
definitional conversion laws required by a dependent quoter.

## A precise coherence obstruction, not a PHOAS impossibility

:::Theorem[Faithful normal syntax cannot also be a semantic type index]
Let a fixed externally indexed domain contain the two closed original types

$$
T_1=(\lambda X:\Ur0.X)\,\Ur0,
\qquad T_0=\Ur0.
$$

No one map $Q$ on that domain can simultaneously have normal outputs,
be injective on literal syntax, and preserve original beta conversion.
The same holds if a separate conversion-preserving index map $J$ is
required to satisfy $Q(T)\bconv J(T)$ pointwise.
:::

:::Proof
Both types inhabit $\Ur0$, and $T_1\bto T_0$ while $T_1\ne T_0$.
Conversion preservation makes their normal images convertible, hence equal
by original confluence. Injectivity gives the contradictory source equality.
For the two-map variant, pointwise conversion transfers conversion preservation
from $J$ to $Q$.
:::

`PHOASConversion` proves the statement at any target scope and external
index. Its two witnesses already fit one independently specified domain:
type rank zero, literal ceiling zero, at most five nodes, and sufficient
redex bound six. Increasing those indices cannot repair the three
simultaneous requirements once both witnesses occur at the same index.
The argument does **not** force two genuinely different maps to agree.
Canonical semantic indices plus faithful syntax codes, or another explicit
coherence mechanism, lie outside the obstruction.

There is also a concrete source regression: the neutral PHOAS quotes of
$\lambda x:o.(\lambda y:o.y)x$ and $\lambda x:o.x$ are provably not
convertible, although the sources differ by one beta step. This is expected
of faithful quotation, not a defect in its interpretation theorem.
For dependent syntax, a quotation map therefore cannot simply be reused
unchanged as the map interpreting type conversion. This is the precise
obstruction established here; arbitrary indexed dependent PHOAS has not
been ruled out.

# Object-language Tarski codes and dependent transport

The canonical metalevel algebra suggests keeping semantic type indices
separate from faithful syntax. A constructive next step is to make the
necessary local transports **actual original PTS terms**. This section
records the local operations used by the full construction later in the note.

## Rank-indexed equality and two-way casts

For $A:\Ur a$ and $u,v:A$, define the Leibniz transport type

$$
\mathsf{Eq}_b(A,u,v)
 =\Pi P:(A\to\Ur b).P\,u\to P\,v,
\qquad
\mathsf{refl}_b(A,u)
 =\lambda P:(A\to\Ur b).\lambda z:P\,u.z.
$$

`IndexedEquality` constructs these original annotated terms and proves

$$
\mathsf{Eq}_b(A,u,v):\Ur{\max(a+2,b+1)},
\qquad
\mathsf{refl}_b(A,u):\mathsf{Eq}_b(A,u,u).
$$

The explicit transport $e\,P\,z$ has type $P\,v$ when
$z:P\,u$, and reflexivity transport reduces to the literal $z$.
There are also closed polymorphic reflexivity programs, exact substitution
laws and normality results. These are not new equality primitives.

Predicate ranks matter. The familiar impredicative proof of symmetry
cannot simply instantiate $P$ with this equality type at the same level:
the equality type itself has a larger sort. For the casts needed here,
use $S,T:\Ur c$ and a witness

$$
e:\mathsf{Eq}_{c+1}(\Ur c,S,T).
$$

Its type has sort $\Ur{c+2}$. Both arrow motives below have values
of sort $\Ur{c+1}$, so the following are well-typed:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf{cast}^{+}_{c,S}(e)
 &=e\,(\lambda X:\Ur c.S\to X)\,(\lambda x:S.x):S\to T,\\
\mathsf{cast}^{-}_{c,S}(e)
 &=e\,(\lambda X:\Ur c.X\to S)\,(\lambda x:S.x):T\to S.
\end{aligned}
$$

`TarskiCasts.casts_typed` proves these application typings directly.
For reflexivity evidence both functions reduce to $\lambda x:S.x$;
applied casts therefore return the original argument, without eta.
Abstract neutral evidence instead blocks computation. If the type,
evidence and argument are normal and the evidence is not lambda-headed,
the applied casts are normal. This is a **local** normality theorem;
normality of every generated dependent annotation is not being assumed.

`ClosedTarskiCasts` closes these operations over $S$, $T$ and $e$.
For each external $c$ the actual closed programs are normal, their types
have sort $\Ur{c+3}$, and their application typings and beta specialization
are proved. Thus transport is an internal program component, not merely
an external proof that two source types have the same normal form.

## Dependent product and universe codes

For each needed external rank $a$, consider object parameters

$$
C_a:\Ur0,\qquad \mathsf{El}_a:C_a\to\Ur a.
$$

These are semantic type-code carriers, not the faithful homogeneous
raw-HOAS carrier. An arbitrary inhabitant is not asserted to be generated
syntax. For $c=\max(a+1,b)$, the product-code operation has type

$$
p_{a,b}:\Pi A:C_a.(\mathsf{El}_a A\to C_b)\to C_c.
$$

`TarskiProducts` proves that this operation type inhabits $\Ur{a+2}$.
For $A:C_a$ and $B:\mathsf{El}_a A\to C_b$, set

$$
X=\Pi x:\mathsf{El}_a A.\mathsf{El}_b(Bx),
\qquad Y=\mathsf{El}_c(p_{a,b}\,A\,B).
$$

Both $X$ and $Y$ inhabit $\Ur c$. An explicit object witness
$e:\mathsf{Eq}_{c+1}(\Ur c,X,Y)$ gives dependent operations

$$
\mathsf{Abs}_e(f)=\mathsf{cast}^{+}_{c,X}(e)\,f:Y,
\qquad
\mathsf{App}_e(g,u)=\mathsf{cast}^{-}_{c,X}(e)\,g\,u:
\mathsf{El}_b(Bu),
$$

where $f:X$, $g:Y$ and $u:\mathsf{El}_a A$.
The family $B$ is genuinely dependent. Original typing of both operations
and the exact product-law rank $c+2$ are proved. The law is an ordinary
typed argument, not a metatheoretic assertion that arbitrary decoders
commute definitionally with products.

Universe codes similarly have $u_a:C_0$ and a law

$$
\mathsf{Eq}_1(\Ur0,\mathsf{El}_0(u_a),C_a):\Ur2.
$$

The two casts connect the decoded universe with its code carrier.
`TarskiUniverses` proves formation and substitution naturality, as well
as actual identity witnesses for **every external rank**, not merely
finitely many computed examples.

:::Theorem[Identity realization of the local Tarski operations]
Specialize

$$
C_a=\Ur a,\qquad
\mathsf{El}_a=\lambda X:\Ur a.X,\qquad u_a=\Ur a,
$$

and

$$
p_{a,b}=\lambda A:\Ur a.\lambda B:(A\to\Ur b).\Pi x:A.Bx.
$$

These are actual originally typed terms. Universe laws are inhabited by
reflexivity using proved beta conversion. For each originally typed
$A:\Ur a$, $B:A\to\Ur b$, $f:\Pi x:A.Bx$ and $u:A$,
both sides of the product law reduce to $\Pi x:A.Bx$; reflexivity at that
common product has the required law type by original conversion.
The specialized casted abstraction and application are originally typed
at $\Pi x:A.Bx$ and $B\,u$, respectively, and reduce to the literal
$f$ and $f\,u$.
:::

`TarskiIdentity` proves the theorem by direct typing and actual beta
reductions. It neither assumes the product law nor projects a precomputed
normal semantic value. The closed identity product constructor and the
closed universe-law witnesses are normal; specializing abstract decoded
types may introduce administrative redexes, which are handled by proved
conversion rather than subject expansion.

## A finite dependent signature and its actual identity instantiation

The local declarations can now be assembled into an actual finite original
context. Fix $k$. Use carriers through rank $k+1$, but product operations
only for $a,b\leq k$. Then $c=\max(a+1,b)\leq k+1$ for every included
pair. Requiring closure under products at **every** available carrier rank
would instead keep demanding a further rank; the finite interface makes
no such requirement. A sufficient literal ceiling is also still needed
for a source containing $\Ur r$: its result type $\Ur0$ alone does not
bound $r$.

The context is built in six dependent layers. Within each layer, every
declaration refers only to the earlier layers; explicit weakening places
it at its actual original de Bruijn scope.

$$
\begin{array}{lccc}
\text{Fields} & \text{External indices} & \text{Number} & \text{Declaration sort}\\
\hline
C_r & 0\leq r\leq k+1 & k+2 & 0\\
\mathsf{El}_r & 0\leq r\leq k+1 & k+2 & 1\\
u_r & 0\leq r\leq k+1 & k+2 & 0\\
\text{Universe-law witnesses} & 0\leq r\leq k+1 & k+2 & 2\\
p_{a,b} & 0\leq a,b\leq k & (k+1)^2 & a+2\\
\text{Product-law families} & 0\leq a,b\leq k & (k+1)^2 & c+2
\end{array}
$$

The last field is a single polymorphic witness for each rank pair:

$$
\ell_{a,b}:\Pi A:C_a.\Pi B:(\mathsf{El}_a A\to C_b).
\mathsf{Eq}_{c+1}\bigl(\Ur c,
  \Pi x:\mathsf{El}_a A.\mathsf{El}_b(Bx),
  \mathsf{El}_c(p_{a,b}\,A\,B)\bigr).
$$

Its declaration type has sort $c+2$: this absorbs the two extra binders
because $c\geq a+1$. Thus infinitely many represented dependent families at a fixed
rank pair need only one field, not an infinite context.
`TarskiLawFamily` proves this sort calculation, literal substitution
naturality and direct typing of the two applications $\ell_{a,b}\,A\,B$.
Its actual closed normal identity inhabitant is

$$
\lambda A:\Ur a.\lambda B:(A\to\Ur b).
\mathsf{refl}_{c+1}(\Ur c,\Pi x:A.Bx).
$$

Original conversion assigns it the identity-specialized field type;
its two applications reduce to the literal local reflexivity witness.

:::Theorem[Finite Tarski signature with constructive identity instantiation]
For every $k$, `TarskiPalette` and `TarskiSignature` compute an original
well-formed context $\Gamma_k$ of exactly
$4(k+2)+2(k+1)^2$ declarations. Every declaration is beta-normal, and all
six kinds of field have their stated original lookup typings within the
indicated bounds. There is a computed simultaneous substitution $\sigma_k$
by actual closed identity terms, with a proved original typed-substitution
judgment and exact equations for every field. No algebra-existence or
law-inhabitation premise is supplied.
:::

`TarskiOperations` obtains product evidence by applying the law field;
an individual equality witness is no longer an extra premise of each
operation. Its product codes, casted abstraction and casted application
have original typings in $\Gamma_k$. After actual replacement by
$\sigma_k$, their computation laws include

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sigma_k(p_{a,b}\,A\,B)&\longrightarrow_\beta^*
   \Pi x:\sigma_k A.(\sigma_k B)x,\\
\sigma_k(\mathsf{Abs}(A,B,f))&\longrightarrow_\beta^*\sigma_k f,\\
\sigma_k(\mathsf{App}(A,B,g,u))&\longrightarrow_\beta^*
   (\sigma_k g)(\sigma_k u).
\end{aligned}
$$

The target is the literal substituted input, not an eta expansion or a
precomputed source normal form. These are local operation equations;
$f$ and $g$ have the respective decoded and encoded product types.
The original `TarskiOperations` normality theorem additionally requires a
normal decoded product annotation: a normal lambda-valued $B$ can create
a redex in $B\,x$. The smart binder construction below removes this extra
premise for its local operations. It does not by itself prove global quotation
normality.
The existing telescope machinery also closes any originally typed body
over $\Gamma_k$, preserves normality of a normal body, and types actual
application to all identity arguments before proving substitution recovery.
This generic closure theorem does not construct a body representing an
arbitrary source derivation.

## Sort quotations and scope-correct dependent extensions

Write $w_r$ for the universe-law field from
$\mathsf{El}_0(u_r)$ to $C_r$. `TarskiSorts` constructs the two views

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf{codeToTerm}_r(A)
 &=\mathsf{cast}^{-}_{0,\mathsf{El}_0(u_r)}(w_r)\,A
   :\mathsf{El}_0(u_r) &&(A:C_r),\\
\mathsf{termToCode}_r(t)
 &=\mathsf{cast}^{+}_{0,\mathsf{El}_0(u_r)}(w_r)\,t
   :C_r &&(t:\mathsf{El}_0(u_r)).
\end{aligned}
$$

For $r\leq k+1$ these are originally typed in $\Gamma_k$.
They preserve normality of their inputs and, after actual identity
substitution, reduce to the literal substituted input. These are
specialization laws, not cancellation laws for abstract neutral casts.

In particular the source axiom $\Ur r:\Ur0$ has the normal quotation

$$
\mathsf{sortQuote}_{k,r}=\mathsf{codeToTerm}_0(u_r)
  :\mathsf{El}_0(u_0).
$$

The cast uses $w_0$, not $w_r$, because $u_r:C_0$.
Closing this body over the finite signature gives an actual closed normal
code. Its application to the identity arguments is originally typed at
$\Ur0$ and reduces to the literal $\Ur r$.
`closed_sort_run_typed` proves application typing independently of
recovery, rather than invoking subject expansion. Every literal is covered
by some finite $k$; this theorem concerns the sort fragment, not arbitrary
source derivations.

`TarskiContext` extends the same signature by any finite sequence of
independently sorted original declarations. It constructs the embedding of
the signature variables, the specialized target context, and the actual
typed substitution along this sequence. At each new binder the substitution
is lifted:

$$
\sigma^{+}(0)=0,\qquad
\sigma^{+}(i+1)=\mathord{\uparrow}(\sigma(i)).
$$

Thus fresh variables remain variables; only the inherited signature fields
receive their identity implementations. Both contexts are well-formed,
original typing transports by this substitution, and each inherited field
retains its lookup and identity equation. The signature embedding is
injective. Normality of the extended abstract context requires normality
of the added declarations; normality of its identity-specialized context
is not asserted. Product and cast operations in this module accept arbitrary
terms in the enlarged scope, including terms mentioning the new dependent
variables, and retain original typing and identity recovery. These are
concrete context-extension operations, not yet a translation of every
original source context.

## A normal local dependent binder

The troublesome family application in a decoded product is always to the
freshly bound variable. `TarskiBinders.bound_apply` exploits precisely that
case. In de Bruijn notation define

$$
\mathsf{fresh}(B)=
\begin{cases}
 b,& B=\lambda D.b,\\
 (\mathord{\uparrow}B)\,0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
$$

Then $(\mathord{\uparrow}B)\,0\longrightarrow_\beta^*\mathsf{fresh}(B)$,
and normal $B$ implies normal $\mathsf{fresh}(B)$. The lambda case merely
removes the administrative fresh-variable application; it performs no
arbitrary argument substitution. Renaming commutes exactly with this
operation. Replacement has the correct one-way computation law

$$
\sigma^{+}(\mathsf{fresh}(B))\longrightarrow_\beta^*
  \mathsf{fresh}(\sigma(B)),
$$

not a claimed literal equality: a neutral family may become a lambda.

Replace the decoded product annotation by

$$
X^{\mathrm{smart}}=
 \Pi x:\mathsf{El}_a A.
 (\mathord{\uparrow}\mathsf{El}_b)\,\mathsf{fresh}(B).
$$

The original decoded product reduces to this one. Original conversion
therefore gives the existing law field the required equality type; no new
law witness is assumed. Under the original input typing premises and
$a,b\leq k$, the resulting smart abstraction and application are originally
typed. They are normal whenever their respective inputs
$A,B,f$ or $A,B,g,u$ are normal. In particular there is no separate
normal-decoded-annotation premise. Their identity specializations recover
the literal substituted $f$ and $g\,u$.

There is also a genuine local binder introduction. For $a,b\leq k$ and
$A:C_a$ in $\Gamma_k$, suppose in $\Gamma_k,x:\mathsf{El}_a A$ that

$$
B_0:C_b,\qquad t:\mathsf{El}_b B_0.
$$

Here the displayed signature fields are weakened into the extended scope.
Set $B=\lambda x:\mathsf{El}_a A.B_0$ and
$f=\lambda x:\mathsf{El}_a A.t$. The smart abstraction of $f$ has
type $\mathsf{El}_c(p_{a,b}\,A\,B)$ and is normal when $A,B_0,t$ are
normal. Its checked recovery target is the literal substituted lambda

$$
\lambda x:\sigma_k(\mathsf{El}_a A).\sigma_k^{+}(t),
$$

including that substituted decoded annotation, not an already contracted
annotation $\sigma_k(A)$. For fixed $k,a,b$, equality of two such binder
terms implies equality of all three supplied inputs $A,B_0,t$. On normal
inputs this also holds for beta conversion. The supplied family remains
present in the neutral law application even though the smart annotation
contracts its administrative application. `binder_inputs_injective` and
`binder_conversion_injective` establish this **local** fidelity, not
injectivity of a full source-derivation translation.
`ScopedTarski` now lifts these smart operations to any independently typed
variable embedding of the finite signature. In particular, a concrete
dependent context spine can be extended by $x:\mathsf{El}_a A$; the child
environment and its lifted identity substitution are computed, not assumed.
`nested_binder_typed` applies the binder rule at arbitrary depth using this
actual child environment. Normality, literal identity recovery, exact
renaming and weakening, and fixed-index local input/conversion injectivity
are retained. Sort quotations and the code/term views also work in these
scopes. This resolves the fixed-scope restriction of the earlier local
interface. It does not yet translate an arbitrary source derivation: the
represented domain, family and body remain independently supplied inputs.

## Composing internal equality evidence

`IndexedCoherence` constructs two further original terms. For
$e:\mathsf{Eq}_b(A,u,v)$ and $f:\mathsf{Eq}_b(A,v,w)$, composition is

$$
\lambda P:(A\to\Ur b).\lambda z:P\,u.f\,P\,(e\,P\,z)
  :\mathsf{Eq}_b(A,u,w).
$$

For an ordinary nondependent map $g:A\to B$, congruence is

$$
\lambda P:(B\to\Ur b).e\,(\lambda x:A.P(gx))
  :\mathsf{Eq}_b(B,gu,gv).
$$

Both preserve the predicate level $b$, and commute literally with
substitution. Composing two reflexivity witnesses or mapping reflexivity
reduces to literal reflexivity, including the latter's annotation.
For arbitrary evidence, the identity laws are proved **after application**
to a motive and an argument; no eta contraction of
$\lambda P.\lambda z.e\,P\,z$ to $e$ is claimed.
These witnesses supply composition and nondependent congruence, not an
extensionality principle for dependent families.

## Predicate levels for reversing and joining evidence

An internal forward join must still reverse its second half. The equality
encoding does not silently supply symmetry at the same predicate level.
For $A:\Ur a$ and desired output level $d$, set

$$
h(a,d)=\max(a+2,d+1).
$$

This is the formation rank of $\mathsf{Eq}_d(A,u,v)$.
`IndexedEqualityLevels` constructs the actual original terms

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf{lower}_d(e)
 &=e\,(\lambda x:A.\mathsf{Eq}_d(A,u,x))\,
       \mathsf{refl}_d(A,u),\\
\mathsf{reverse}_d(e)
 &=e\,(\lambda x:A.\mathsf{Eq}_d(A,x,u))\,
       \mathsf{refl}_d(A,u).
\end{aligned}
$$

Given $e:\mathsf{Eq}_{h(a,d)}(A,u,v)$, their respective types are
$\mathsf{Eq}_d(A,u,v)$ and $\mathsf{Eq}_d(A,v,u)$.
Both reduce to literal level-$d$ reflexivity when $e$ is reflexivity.
For $e:u\sim w$ and $f:v\sim w$ at level $h(a,d)$, composing
$\mathsf{lower}_d(e)$ with $\mathsf{reverse}_d(f)$ gives
$\mathsf{Eq}_d(A,u,v)$. These constructors commute literally with
replacement and are normal on normal inputs with non-lambda-headed
evidence. They are not asserted normal after arbitrary specialization.

For equality between types $X,Y:\Ur c$, the carrier is $\Ur c:\Ur0$.
Consequently, predicate level $c+2$ suffices to reverse into the level
$c+1$ used by the casts above. This is explicit rank accounting, not
cumulativity or a claim that any larger predicate level is interchangeable.
Since forward composition preserves the predicate level, one can compose
each forward path before reversing once; this operation does not itself
require a rank increase at every path step. Generating the input witnesses
and proving that a chosen finite palette supplies their exact levels remain
separate obligations.

## Synchronized evidence for dependent application

For $A:\Ur a$ and $B:A\to\Ur b$, define graph equality by

$$
\mathsf{GEq}_d(A,B,u,y,v,z)=
 \Pi P:(\Pi x:A.Bx\to\Ur d).P\,u\,y\to P\,v\,z.
$$

Here $y:Bu$ and $z:Bv$ may live in different fibers. Its exact sort is
$\Ur{\max(\max(a+2,b+2),d+1)}$. `DependentEquality` constructs
reflexivity, direct graph transport, and synchronized dependent congruence:
for $f:\Pi x:A.Bx$ and $e:\mathsf{Eq}_d(A,u,v)$,

$$
\lambda P.\,e\,(\lambda x:A.P\,x\,(f\,x))
 :\mathsf{GEq}_d(A,B,u,f\,u,v,f\,v).
$$

The predicate annotation on $P$ is the one displayed in the definition.
This preserves level $d$ and needs neither function extensionality nor a
proof-dependent eliminator. At reflexivity it reduces to literal graph
reflexivity, including its annotation. For a fixed index, ordinary evidence
$e:\mathsf{Eq}_d(Bu,y,z)$ also gives

$$
\lambda P.\,e\,(P\,u):\mathsf{GEq}_d(A,B,u,y,u,z).
$$

Both constructions commute with replacement. Their normality theorems
retain the necessary non-lambda-head guards: a normal lambda-valued family
$B$ still introduces a redex in the predicate annotation $B\,x$.
This graph evidence does not establish equality in the target fiber between
$f\,v$ and the result of a separately chosen transport of $f\,u$.
Nor does it establish function equality from arbitrary pointwise evidence.
It tracks the dependent index and value together.

## Binder congruence through a common map

There is a useful binder case that needs no pointwise-to-function principle.
Let $D:\Ur s$, $X:\Ur a$, $C:\Ur c$, and suppose
$b(z,x):C$ in the independently typed context $z:D,x:X$.
The type $X$ and codomain $C$ do not depend on $z$, but the body may
depend on both variables. Define

$$
H=\lambda z:D.\lambda x:X.b(z,x).
$$

For an **outer** witness $e:\mathsf{Eq}_d(D,u,v)$, ordinary map
congruence constructs

$$
\mathsf{Eq}_d\bigl(X\to C,
  \lambda x:X.b(u,x),\lambda x:X.b(v,x)\bigr).
$$

`BinderCongruence` proves this at sort
$\Ur{\max(\max(a+1,c)+2,d+1)}$, with actual binder-aware substitution,
replacement naturality and literal reflexivity computation. Taking
$X=\mathsf{El}_a A$ and $C=C_b$, then mapping by $p_{a,b}\,A$, yields
internal equality of the corresponding product codes. At the actual
identity product constructor and reflexive input, its witness reduces to
literal reflexivity at $\Pi x:A.b(u,x)$, without eta conversion.

This handles families obtained from a common map and one witness outside
the binder. It does not handle arbitrary $x$-dependent evidence $e_x$.
The generated proof terms can contain administrative beta redexes;
full normality is not claimed for this constructor.

## An independently realized beta-law interface

The neutral-cast counterexample does not prevent adding explicit internal
evidence to a finite algebra, provided its identity interpretation is
constructed independently. `TarskiBeta` makes this option concrete. Put
$c=\max(a+1,b)$ and $e=\ell_{a,b}\,A\,B$. For
$f:\Pi x:\mathsf{El}_a A.\mathsf{El}_b(Bx)$ and
$u:\mathsf{El}_a A$, the proposed field concludes

$$
\mathsf{Eq}_d\bigl(\mathsf{El}_b(Bu),
  \mathsf{App}(\mathsf{Abs}(f),u),f\,u\bigr).
$$

Here $\mathsf{Abs}$ and $\mathsf{App}$ are the original two casts using
$e$, not silently cancelling operations. Binding $A,B,f,u$ gives an
original PTS type of sort

$$
\Ur{\max(c+1,\max(b+2,d+1))}.
$$

The declaration is normal when its carrier, decoder and law fields are
variables. Under the actual identity interpretation it has the closed
normal inhabitant

$$
\lambda A:\Ur a.\lambda B:(A\to\Ur b).
\lambda f:(\Pi x:A.Bx).\lambda u:A.
  \mathsf{refl}_d(Bu,f\,u).
$$

Its typing is proved at the literal specialized field type by original
conversion. Four actual applications reduce to literal reflexivity,
including its annotation. Formation of this field is not a proof that
an arbitrary product-law witness already entails it.

`TarskiBetaSignature` adds one such field for each $a,b,d\leq k$ to
the unchanged six-layer signature. The resulting actual context has

$$
4(k+2)+2(k+1)^2+(k+1)^3
$$

declarations. Its original well-formedness, normal declarations, variable
lookups and constructive typed identity substitution are proved. Applying
a field to independently typed $A,B,f,u$ yields the displayed internal
equality; substituting the actual identity fields reduces this application
to reflexivity on the literal substituted inputs. The inherited signature
embedding supplies the scoped smart operations' typing and normality
interface. These finite beta laws neither add a primitive to PTS-rk nor
prove the full source-conversion theorem. In particular, they do not yet
supply congruence under dependent binders or a family-level equality from
pointwise evidence.

`TarskiGraphBeta` connects this actual finite field to graph equality.
It constructs the decoded fiber
$F=\lambda x:\mathsf{El}_a A.\mathsf{El}_b(Bx)$ and uses the
fixed-fiber bridge to obtain

$$
\mathsf{GEq}_d\bigl(\mathsf{El}_a A,F,
 u,\mathsf{App}(\mathsf{Abs}(f),u),u,f\,u\bigr).
$$

Typing uses the real field lookup and original conversion for
$F\,u\longrightarrow_\beta\mathsf{El}_b(Bu)$; no new equality
premise replaces the field. Actual identity substitution reduces the
witness to graph reflexivity at the literal substituted domain, family,
index and value. In particular the substituted lambda family is retained,
not eta-contracted to $B$. Its filled witness is originally typed
directly from the constructed typed substitution. This literal graph
specialization is not asserted normal: its lambda-valued fiber creates
the annotation redex just noted.

## Executable full-source derivations and canonical conversion traces

`FullSource` gives computational evidence in Rocq's `Type` for the exact
original six typing rules, single beta steps, forward reductions and
bidirectional conversion paths. The terms, contexts and lambda annotations
are the original syntax. Inhabitation of each datatype is proved equivalent
to the corresponding original relation; the typing equivalence covers all
original judgments, without a well-formed-context restriction. Conversion
paths may have untyped intermediate terms, just as the original relation
permits. A computable finite ceiling bounds the supplied derivation's
literal labels, its explicitly recorded rule ranks and every conversion
intermediate's literals. This is not yet a sufficient target-quoter budget
or a bound on every inferred semantic rank.

The distinction between supplied data and erased proofs matters. The
equivalence is propositional: no function extracts a computational
derivation from an arbitrary original `Prop` proof. Subsequent executable
operations consume supplied derivation data.

`FullConversion` computes forward traces for every greedy pass and for
the existing automatic open-context normalizer. It compares the two
computed endpoints by structural equality of annotated syntax. Success
returns actual traces

$$
t\longrightarrow_\beta^* v
\quad\text{and}\quad
u\longrightarrow_\beta^* v.
$$

Success always implies original conversion, even on raw inputs. In a
well-formed original context, for independently originally typed $t:T$
and $u:U$, the comparison succeeds exactly when $t=_\beta u$. Its common
endpoint is normal, and every stage of each forward trace retains its
starting term's original type, $T$ or $U$ respectively. No claim of
completeness on arbitrary untyped syntax is made.

`CanonicalSource.canonicalize` recursively transforms a supplied full
source derivation in a well-formed context. At each conversion rule it
replaces the supplied zigzag by this computed forward join, leaving
**all source terms, types, contexts and annotations literally unchanged**.
The function obtains its data from the executable comparison; validity
and the supplied conversion proof only exclude the failure branch.
An independent output theorem certifies normal common endpoints and
originally typed intermediate stages at every conversion node.
Every original typing derivation in a well-formed context has such
certified data. Thus a future translation can traverse typed forward
paths without interpreting arbitrary untyped conversion intermediates.
These are metalevel source certificates, not object-language conversion
witnesses or a target quotation. Literal source preservation leaves the
existing syntax-injective HOAS coding available, but does not prove
injectivity of a dependent PHOAS translation by itself.

`SourceSubstitution` now supplies an executable source-data operation needed
for this induction. A pointwise substitution carries independent `FullSource`
derivations for its variable images; inhabitation of such data is equivalent
to the original typed-substitution relation, again only propositionally.
Structural renaming, weakening, binder lifting and substitution transform
derivations and their explicit conversion paths. Given body and argument
derivation data, `beta_contractum_derivation` computes a derivation of the
literal $b[u/x]:B[u/x]$, including substituted annotations. Supplying the
domain formation also constructs the typed redex and proves its original
beta step and contractum typing. This does not infer derivation data from
the propositional stage typings above, or by itself construct the target
quotation's substitution witness. Typed constructor-view substitution is
supplied later.

## Why the local signature alone is insufficient

The finite signature and its identity substitution do not alone construct
a quotation of every original typing derivation with
generated substitution and source-conversion coherence. In particular,
arbitrary proof-headed casts do not definitionally cancel. A pointwise
Leibniz witness for two families also cannot simply be used as an equality
of the object-language functions expected by congruence of $p_{a,b}$.

`TarskiRoundtrip` makes the first limitation precise for the existing cast
definitions. In the actual well-formed original context

$$
S:\Ur c,\quad T:\Ur c,\quad
e:\mathsf{Eq}_{c+1}(\Ur c,S,T),\quad x:S,
$$

both $x$ and
$\mathsf{cast}^{-}_{c,S}(e)(\mathsf{cast}^{+}_{c,S}(e)\,x)$
are originally typed at $S$. They are distinct normal terms, hence are not
beta-convertible. Extending this context by $K:S\to\Ur0$ gives two
normal, well-typed, nonconvertible type indices by applying $K$ to them.
At actual reflexivity evidence the same roundtrip instead reduces to $x$.
This counterexample forbids treating these neutral casts as a definitional
retraction. It does not exclude internal equality evidence, additional
independently realized coherence fields, different casts, or full dependent
PHOAS.

There is a checked beta-only regression. With $D=\Ur0\to\Ur0$, the
closed normal functions

$$
F=\lambda f:D.f,\qquad G=\lambda f:D.\lambda x:\Ur0.f\,x
$$

have the same original type but are not beta-convertible. Nevertheless,
$F\,f\,x$ and $G\,f\,x$ both reduce to $f\,x$. In an original
well-formed context containing $f:D$ and $K:D\to\Ur0$, the types
$K\,f$ and $K\,(\lambda x:\Ur0.f\,x)$ are likewise normal,
well-typed and nonconvertible. `EtaBoundary` proves these facts from
normal-form uniqueness. They forbid silently replacing beta conversion
by pointwise agreement; they do not rule out full dependent PHOAS or
prove that every possible internal equality witness is absent.

A construction based only on these local laws would need to translate the executable source data
using the arbitrary-scope sort and smart binder operations, and generate
the needed internal substitution and conversion witnesses along the
computed typed forward paths. It must establish their identity computation
laws, global annotated normality and source-syntax fidelity. The finite
beta fields and their graph specialization address a local
application/abstraction law. Common-map binder congruence and synchronized
dependent application evidence do not give dependent congruence for every
source reduction. Metalevel canonicalization, raw
syntax fidelity and the local casts remain separate proved ingredients,
not substitutes for that induction. The checked equality-layer construction
below completes full-source coverage by a different repair interface.

## Contextual beta laws and a finite telescope interface

The specific cast-beta equation under a dependent telescope is now
constructed. This is an independently realized law family, not a rule
turning arbitrary pointwise equality into function equality.
Let

$$
\Delta=(x_1:X_1,\ldots,x_m:X_m),\qquad
\Gamma,x_1:X_1,\ldots,x_{i-1}:X_{i-1}\vdash X_i:\Ur{r_i}.
$$

`ContextualTelescope` records these original formation judgments separately
from any quotation property. Its closures are literal annotated products
and lambdas. For a body type of rank $b$, product closure has exact rank

$$
R(\vec r,b)=\max\bigl(b,\max(\{0\}\cup\{r_i+1:1\leq i\leq m\})\bigr).
$$

The module proves original closure typing, renaming, substitution and
annotation-sensitive normality. In particular, closing a normal body is
normal precisely when every retained domain annotation is normal.

For the previously defined cast operations, `ContextualBeta` forms

$$
\mathsf{Eq}_d\bigl(\Pi\Delta.\mathsf{El}_b(B\,u),
 \lambda\Delta.\mathsf{castApp}(e,\mathsf{castAbs}(e,f),u),
 \lambda\Delta.f\,u\bigr).
$$

Its sort rank is $\max(R(\vec r,b)+2,d+1)$. The typing proof uses
the supplied original typings of the carriers, decoders, product code,
law, function and argument. At the actual identity interpretation, the
two endpoints reduce to the same literal multi-lambda. Reflexivity at
that term is constructed and typed independently, then assigned the
abstract equation type by original type conversion. No subject expansion
of the witness, eta rule or function-extensionality assumption is used.

`TelescopeBetaFamily` additionally binds four object arguments: the
carrier family $A$, dependent code family $B$, function family $f$, and
argument family $u$, each abstracted over $\Delta$. Writing $A_\Delta$
for application to all variables of $\Delta$, the conclusion uses
$e=\ell\,A_\Delta\,B_\Delta$, $f_\Delta$ and $u_\Delta$.
The full four-argument family has formation rank

$$
\max\bigl(\max(\{0\}\cup\{r_i+1\})+2,
           a+2,b+2,d+1\bigr).
$$

Its identity implementation uses plain, reduced lambda annotations and
ends in reflexivity at $\lambda\Delta.f_\Delta\,u_\Delta$.
Direct original typing, full annotated normality under normal domains,
substitution and literal four-argument specialization are proved
separately. The one-binder `ContextualBetaFamily` also quantifies its domain
$X:\Ur s$ and has exact rank $\max(s+3,a+2,b+2,d+1)$.

Keeping a concrete $\Delta$ in an external field index would not give a
finite interface. `UniformTelescope` instead generates bound domain
parameters

$$
X_1:\Ur{r_1},\quad
X_2:\Pi x_1:X_1.\Ur{r_2},\quad\ldots,\quad
X_m:\Pi x_1:X_1.\cdots\Pi x_{m-1}:X_{m-1}\,\vec x_{<m-1}.\Ur{r_m}.
$$

The generated telescope uses neutral applications $X_i\,\vec x_{<i}$
as its domain annotations. Its parameter context and its domains have
original formation and full normality, proved for every rank vector.
Parameter $X_i$ has declaration-formation rank
$R((r_1,\ldots,r_{i-1}),0)$, since $\Ur{r_i}:\Ur0$.
Renaming and substitution commute with the generator exactly.

`UniformTelescopeRecovery.realize` computes actual parameter values from
any supplied concrete telescope: each declaration is lambda-closed over
its literal preceding domains. Independent original formation suffices to
prove the resulting substitution is typed. Instantiated generated domains
beta-reduce annotation by annotation to the concrete telescope, expressed
in the generator's accumulated scope order. A further theorem recovers
the exact original product closure ending in any universe $\Ur s$.
This is genuine domain-parameter coverage. The subsequent
`OpenTelescopeRecovery` module also transports arbitrary open bodies and
literal lambda closures, with original typing in both directions across
the realized telescope. `ConcreteContextualBeta` applies the actual finite
field to independently typed open arguments and proves literal identity
recovery, normality under explicit normality guards, and local input
injectivity. Integration with recursive quotation is a further obligation.

`UniformContextualBeta` abstracts the four-argument family over these
domain parameters. Its syntax depends externally only on $\vec r,a,b,d$,
not on the concrete domain terms. With all these ranks bounded by $k$,
its declaration has formation rank at most $k+3$. Its actual identity
value is a closed normal original PTS term; substitution invariance,
original typing and normality are separate proofs.

Length must be bounded independently. `ContextualBudgets` proves that
vectors of arbitrarily large length can have closure rank $1$: take
arbitrarily many zero-ranked declarations. Consequently no bound on this
rank alone bounds telescope length. It also gives an executable complete
enumeration of the vectors of length at most $L$ with entries at most $k$,
of cardinality $\sum_{j=0}^{L}(k+1)^j$.
`TelescopeBetaSignature` adds exactly

$$
(k+1)^3\sum_{j=0}^{L}(k+1)^j
$$

contextual fields to the existing finite beta signature. Executable lookup
recovers each bounded vector and rank triple. Context formation, full
annotation normality, field typing, the actual typed identity substitution,
and closed packaging/application recovery are proved. Every finite vector
and triple has a computed sufficient budget; this descriptor coverage is
not yet coverage of translated full source derivations.
The separate one-binder signature retains local input-syntax and conversion
injectivity. Existing faithful raw-HOAS and STLC quotations are unchanged.

These results discharge the formation, independent identity-realization,
and finite-budget obligations for this contextual cast-beta family. They
do not complete dependent source-reduction coherence: assembling compatible
context/type/term/substitution translations is a separate obligation, supplied
by the later checked-layer construction.

## Checked equality layers and finite constructor views

A second construction strategy uses an acyclic sequence of independently
realized equality-only signature extensions. Suppose a previously formed
signature $\mathcal S$ has a closed, originally typed identity filling
$\iota$, and $\Delta$ is an originally formed local dependent telescope.
An extension may contain the field

$$
e:\Pi\Delta.\operatorname{Eq}_d(C,u,v)
$$

only after $C$, $u$ and $v$ are independently typed in the old context,
all field annotations are normal, and the actual filled endpoints
$u[\iota]$ and $v[\iota]$ are beta-convertible. These are decidable checks
on ordinary annotated PTS syntax, not tests for quotation success.
`OriginalTypecheck` supplies executable inference and checking with
soundness and completeness for the unchanged original typing judgment in
well-formed contexts. `BoundedSyntaxEnumeration` enumerates every syntax
tree within explicit node and literal ceilings, including annotations.

`RealizedEquality` constructs the equality realizer using the common normal
form of the filled endpoints. `ContextualEqualityRealizer` closes it over
the local telescope and normalizes this eraser program. This does not
normalize the represented payload. Its lambda-spine invariant proves that
application to all telescope arguments, a motive and a payload reduces to
that literal payload. `ContextualEqualityExtension` and `CodeAlignment`
therefore provide actual originally typed code alignment, including under
dependent binders, with normal abstract applications and literal filled
recovery. No arbitrary semantic-value fields are added.

`CheckedEqualityFields` constructs a complete finite checked layer over a
fixed preceding context. `CheckedContextualFields` extends its candidate
syntax to locally dependent telescopes and proves actual field lookup,
original formation, normality, closed typed filling and literal transport
erasure. Its coverage theorem bounds telescope length, node counts and
literal labels separately. The whole interpreter below is indexed by a
finite vector of these numerical layer budgets; each layer refers only to
earlier layers. This is a stronger numerical index than rank alone. Neither
finite candidate coverage nor the existing rank bounds already establish
coverage of all translated source derivations.

The source recursion must also terminate without re-quoting an ever larger
generated type derivation. `SourceValidity` computes original result-type
formation, but naively recursing on that output is unsuitable: substitution
can enlarge it, and $\Ur0:\Ur0$ creates a terminal-sort cycle.
`QuotationTree` instead keeps a finite literal source-constructor tree
separate from its target root cache. A Pi view retains independently typed
domain and codomain children even when conversion has changed that cache.
`QuotationCache`, `QuotationViewRenaming` and `QuotationViewSubstitution`
make this separation typed. Their executable structural substitution keeps
the root cache literally substituted. If a source variable is replaced by
a Pi term, it takes the argument's stored constructor shape and checks the
new root using original typing and uniqueness of sort ranks; it does not
call quotation on a synthesized source derivation. Under a fresh binder,
the actual source and target realizations are extended constructively.
The direct codomain operation produces a view of literal $B[u/x]$ with
root cache exactly $B^q[q/x]$. Normal neutral substitution preserves full
normality, including all annotations.

## A dependent decoder with an ordinary represented-type argument

`DependentProgramDecoder` separates the decoder interface from the structural
all-source quotation construction below. Let $\mathcal S$ be a finite originally
formed closed signature, with declaration-rank vector $\vec r$, a code
carrier $C:\Ur0$, a decoder $E:C\to\Ur s$, and an independently typed
closed filling $\iota$. Define

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf{TyProg}&=\Pi\mathcal S.C,\\
\mathsf{TmProg}(P)&=\Pi\mathcal S.E\,(P\,\mathcal S),\\
\mathsf{Result}(P)&=E[\iota]\,(P\,\iota),\\
\mathsf{Dec}&=\lambda P:\mathsf{TyProg}.
  \lambda Q:\mathsf{TmProg}(P).Q\,\iota.
\end{aligned}
$$

Here $P$ is an ordinary object-language argument, not an external source
type or typing-derivation index. In the numerical family below,
$\mathcal S,C,E,\iota$ are determined by its numerical signature index and
result rank. `TelescopeInstantiation` proves the original typed mixed
substitution that retains $P,Q$ while filling the signature parameters.
The decoder is directly originally typed; no subject expansion is used.
Its type has formation rank

$$
\max\bigl(R(\vec r,0)+1,\ R(\vec r,s)+1,\ s\bigr).
$$

If the signature annotations, $C$, the neutral $E$, and all filling values
are normal, the decoder itself is fully normal. Given independently typed
open codes $A^q:C$ and $t^q:E\,A^q$, their actual lambda closures give
$P$ and $Q$. The typing of $Q:\mathsf{TmProg}(P)$ uses conversion of its
type, whose application spine reduces to the original decoded code type;
the payload is unchanged. Two administrative beta steps and signature
application yield

$$
\mathsf{Dec}\,(\lambda\mathcal S.A^q)\,
 (\lambda\mathcal S.t^q)\ \to^*\ t^q[\iota].
$$

Thus any subsequently constructed literal recovery of $t^q$ is preserved.
Likewise, when $E[\iota]$ is the type identity and $A^q[\iota]\to^* A$,
the result type reduces to $A$. Closure preserves normality and is
syntactically injective at a fixed signature. These generic decoder
and packaging theorems do not alone provide full dependent quotation;
the following structural construction does. Strong recovery alone would not
prove syntax-level injectivity for distinct beta-convertible sources.

## Faithful packages of actual dependent programs

`FaithfulProgramDecoder` separates evaluation from syntax fidelity. Let
$\mathsf{Tag}_r$ be the existing closed raw HOAS code type, and write
$\langle t\rangle_r$ for its normal, annotation-preserving quotation.
For an actual represented program $Q:V$, define

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf{Pack}_r(V)&=(V\to\mathsf{Tag}_r\to V)\to V,\\
\mathsf{pack}_r(V,Q,t)&=
 \lambda h:(V\to\mathsf{Tag}_r\to V).h\,Q\,\langle t\rangle_r,\\
\mathsf{pick}_r(V)&=\lambda q:V.\lambda z:\mathsf{Tag}_r.q.
\end{aligned}
$$

The faithful decoder first selects $Q$ and then applies the actual
signature filling to it. Thus its computational result is $Q\,\iota$,
not $Q$ itself and not an externally computed normal form of $t$.
Only generic packaging lemmas are reused from `WeakFullInterpretation`;
neither its source normalizer nor its precomputed semantic payload is used.
The normal annotation $V=\Pi\mathcal S.E\,A^q$ is convertible to
$\mathsf{TmProg}(P)$ for $P=\lambda\mathcal S.A^q$. Conversion changes
the package's type, not its payload or annotations.

The faithful decoder's type has exactly the checked formation rank

$$
\max\bigl(R(\vec r,0)+1,
  \max(R(\vec r,s)+2,r+5)+1,\ s\bigr).
$$

Normal actual signature annotations and filling values make the decoder
normal; normal $V,Q$ make the package normal. Equality of two such package
terms implies literal equality of their annotated source terms, even when
their signatures, derivations and source types differ. Conversion of normal
packages has the same consequence by confluence. The tag establishes this
fidelity independently of any possible noninjectivity of conversion repairs.

`ClosedQuotationPackage` combines these results with an independently
constructed dependent quotation judgment. It closes the actual signature,
proves original typings for the type program, faithful term package and
interpreter, and proves reduction to the literal source term and source
type. `NormalEqualityFill` derives the required filling normality for the
actual finite Tarski signature and every checked equality layer; it is not
an extra condition imposed on source terms. These are closing theorems;
the structural construction below supplies the required quotation for
every original derivation.

## Executable rank budgets and literal dependent local scopes

`SourceQuotationBudget` computes a budget from Type-valued original
derivation and context data. It takes maxima over all recursive premises,
their result-type ranks, explicit binder ranks and literal sort labels.
The resulting bound covers the extended contexts in both binder cases
and both premises of application and conversion. Literal labels cannot
be replaced by result-type ranks: $\Ur{37}:\Ur0$ has result-type rank zero,
but its literal ceiling is $37$. This regression is checked explicitly.
Original proposition-valued typing has a coverage theorem; no typing proof
is eliminated from `Prop` to compute quotation data.

`LocalQuotationSpine` keeps source binders in snoc order. A state with
$n$ local binders and $m$ global fields has target scope $n+m$ and literal
source scope $n$. Pushing a binder therefore has scope $n+1$ without
normalizing its source declaration. Global equality layers can grow while
preserving that same literal source context. `LocalSpineNaturality` proves
that the telescope allocator's presentation commutes with such growth;
its proof-only scope casts introduce no object-language constructors.

The execution plan has only three constructors: the empty plan, a request
carrying three numeric bounds, and sequential composition. Its evaluation
uses the actual intermediate realized signature. Flattening yields the
same finite vector of numeric layer budgets. This is a source-independent
index format, not a rank-only uniformity theorem, and no elementary
quotation-cost bound is asserted. The next construction discharges the
full structural source-quotation obligation.

## Full dependent interpretation with numerical signature plans

The structural construction is `RecursiveQuotation.quote_derivation`.
Its inputs are an independent `FullSource.derivation`, original context
data, an actually realized local state and variable environment, and the
computed numerical budget. Its result is a finite plan and a quotation
judgment in the corresponding extended signature. The result judgment
contains independently checked original typing, full normality, a finite
typed constructor view, and reduction of the actual filled program to the
literal annotated source. None of those target properties is an input
assumption about the source derivation.

The recursion has six cases, matching the original rules.

- Sorts and variables use the actual finite interface and variable environment.
- Products and lambdas quote the domain, extend by its normal decoded code,
  and recursively quote the existing body premise under the literal source
  binder. `FullQuotation` proves that global signature growth commutes with
  this local extension.
- Application inverts the stored literal product view, not the possibly
  converted root cache. `ApplicationQuotation` allocates exactly two checked
  layers: one aligns the argument's type code, and one aligns the function's
  root with its constructed product code. Structural substitution of the
  codomain view then gives the actual dependent result type.
- Conversion quotes the rule's existing target-formation premise and
  aligns the original payload with that independently constructed code.
  Its equality realizer erases to the same literal payload.

Quotation of a term whose type is a literal universe uses a separate
nonrecursive terminal repair. It does not recursively quote a generated
validity derivation; in particular, $\Ur0:\Ur0$ creates no recursive cycle.
No untyped intermediate of a source conversion zigzag needs quotation.

Write $I_{r,k,p,s}$ for the family in `IndexedProgramInterpreter`.
Here $k$ is the finite source-interface budget, $p$ a pure numerical
allocation plan, $s$ the result-code rank, and $r$ the raw-tag carrier rank.
These determine the signature, its identity filling and the interpreter.
The represented type $P$ and faithful term program $Q$ are ordinary inputs.

:::Theorem[Full closed original-source interpretation]
For every original judgment $\vdash t:T$ and every tag rank $r$, there
exist finite numerical indices $k,p,s$ and closed original terms $P,Q$
such that $s\le k+1$, $P,Q,I_{r,k,p,s}$ are fully beta-normal, and

$$
\begin{aligned}
\vdash P&:\mathsf{TyProg}_{k,p,s},\\
\vdash Q&:\mathsf{Pack}_r(\mathsf{TmProg}_{k,p,s}(P)),\\
\vdash I_{r,k,p,s}
 &:\Pi P:\mathsf{TyProg}_{k,p,s}.
    \mathsf{Pack}_r(\mathsf{TmProg}_{k,p,s}(P))
       \to\mathsf{Result}_{k,p,s}(P),\\
\vdash I_{r,k,p,s}\,P\,Q&:T,\\
I_{r,k,p,s}\,P\,Q&\to^* t.
\end{aligned}
$$

The interpreter type has the explicit finite formation rank proved above.
Applying the type program to the identity filling reduces to the literal
source type $T$. For the constructed quotation procedure, equality or beta
conversion of two term programs implies literal equality of their annotated
source terms, independently of their derivations and numerical plans.
:::

:::Proof
For supplied Type-valued derivation data, compute `SourceQuotationBudget`,
initialize the independently realized empty-source state, and run structural
quotation. Close its resulting judgment using `ClosedQuotationPackage`.
Actual signature and filling normality follow from the checked layer
construction; the faithful envelope supplies syntax injectivity.
For the propositional coverage theorem, original typing implies inhabitation
of the independent derivation datatype. This witness is used only inside
the final proposition, not eliminated from `Prop` to compute a program.
The endpoints are `FullDependentInterpreter.every_closed_original`,
`closed_type_recovers`, `closed_injective` and
`closed_conversion_injective`.
:::

`SourceClosure` extends coverage to every originally well-formed open
context $\Gamma$. It computes the literal closure
$\lambda\Gamma.t$ and its original derivation, quotes this closed term,
then applies the interpreted closure to the ordinary variables of $\Gamma$.
`FullDependentInterpreter.every_original` proves original result typing
and literal recovery. `program_injective` and
`program_conversion_injective` preserve source syntax at a fixed source
scope, even across different same-length contexts. This open interface
uses explicit closure and reopening; it is not a claim that a fixed closed
head can create arbitrary free variables.

This is a positive **numerical-plan-indexed full-source** result. Plan length
and syntax bounds may grow with the source derivation. It does not establish
one decoder for all terms of a fixed rank, a single all-rank interpreter,
efficient uniform enumeration, or an interpreter for unadorned raw HOAS
without the dependent PHOAS program. The interpreter evaluates that actual
program; the raw tag is used only for fidelity. Extraction and direct Rocq
evaluation are distinct operational claims: proof-only equality transports
can obstruct direct reduction of dependent records, and no general runtime
benchmark follows merely from the structural definition.

A proof-erased OCaml smoke test executes the actual closed sort-quotation
path through this construction and checks an empty allocation plan and
normal type and term programs. Its successful run checks that leaf path,
not the performance of dependent application or large equality layers.

Every originally typed member of this interpreter family is itself a closed
source term, so the coverage theorem applies to it at some finite numerical
index. That index may be larger or different: this consequence is not a
same-index fixed-point or a rank-only self-applicability claim.

# Executable structural quotation for the simply typed fragment

The existing independent source `ChurchSTLC` has a distinguished ground
type variable $o:\Ur0$, simple types $\sigma$, and annotated lambda
terms. It has its own typing rules. Its embedding uses

$$
|o|=o,\qquad |\sigma\to\tau|=|\sigma|\to|\tau|,
\qquad
\operatorname{ord}(\sigma\to\tau)=
\max(\operatorname{ord}(\sigma)+1,\operatorname{ord}(\tau)).
$$

All type codes are beta-normal, and $|\sigma|$ inhabits
$\Ur{\operatorname{ord}(\sigma)}$ in the target context. No source
rule depends on conversion of terms inside types.

Fix a budget $d$ bounding the order of the **function type of every
application** in a source typing derivation. It is not enough to bound
the final result type. For an example entirely within STLC, put
$\sigma_0=o\to o$, $w_0=\lambda z:o.z$,
$\sigma_{N+1}=\sigma_N\to\sigma_0$, and
$w_{N+1}=\lambda x:\sigma_N.w_0$. Then
$(\lambda x:\sigma_N.w_0)\,w_N$ has the fixed result type
$\sigma_0$, while its function and argument orders grow without bound.
The independent relation `bounded_type` adds only this numerical bound
to the original STLC rules; `bounded_type_source` forgets it, and
`every_source_has_budget` proves that every source derivation has some
finite budget. No quotation or recovery property defines the source domain.

Introduce variables $b_0:\Id_0,\ldots,b_d:\Id_d$. The
prequotation is the following derivation-directed structural translation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
q(x)&=x,\\
q(\lambda x:|\sigma|.t)&=\lambda x:|\sigma|.q(t),\\
q(f\,u)&=b_r\,|\sigma\to\tau|\,q(f)\,q(u),
\qquad r=\operatorname{ord}(\sigma\to\tau),
\end{aligned}
$$

where the last case uses the source judgment $f:\sigma\to\tau$.
The formal version supplies all scope changes and binder shifts explicitly.
No application in $q(t)$ has a lambda at its head: each translated
application is headed by a neutral blocker. The remaining annotations
are normal simple-type codes. Thus the quotation is normal for the
**full original PTS reduction**, including annotation reduction.

Typing follows from
$b_r\,|\sigma\to\tau|:(|\sigma|\to|\tau|)\to
(|\sigma|\to|\tau|)$ and the translated subderivations.
Substituting $\id_r$ for each blocker gives strong recovery to the
original annotated source term by induction. Syntactic injectivity is
proved at each fixed budget with a structural eraser for the administrative
blocker pattern. Recovery up to beta conversion alone would not establish
injectivity, since distinct source terms may be beta-convertible.

The Rocq module `RankedQuotation` implements these claims for arbitrary
bounded derivations and explicitly typed blocker contexts. Its original
existence theorem is relational, in Rocq `Prop`.
The theorem `finite_budget_quotation` supplies the actual
finite blocker context, and `finite_package_injective` proves injectivity
of the resulting packages. `every_source_has_normal_representation`
covers every independent STLC derivation at some budget.

The new `ExecutableQuotation` module supplies an executable external
algorithm. Its source derivation is an inductive datatype in Rocq `Type`,
with the independent variable, lambda, and application rules, indexed by
the original annotated source term and its simple type. No constructor
assumes target PTS typing, quotation, or recovery. The theorem
`source_derivation` relates its inhabitation to the earlier independent
`ChurchSTLC.has_type` judgment. This is not an illicit elimination from
a proof in `Prop` into computational data: the executable function consumes
the derivation data explicitly. The coverage theorem remains a proposition;
it is not a source type-inference algorithm.

Structural recursion computes both prequotation and a sufficient budget:

$$
\begin{aligned}
b(D_{\mathrm{var}})&=0, &
b(D_{\mathrm{lam}})&=b(D_{\mathrm{body}}),\\
b(D_{\mathrm{app}})&=
\max\{\operatorname{ord}(\sigma\to\tau),b(D_f),b(D_u)\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The executable prequotation follows exactly the displayed structural
clauses. Its relationship to the existing relation is two-way: a related
prequote exists exactly when some computational source derivation produces
that prequote with budget at most $d$. Conversely, each supplied derivation
produces a related prequote at every sufficient budget. Thus executable
normality, original PTS typing, and strong recovery reuse the proved
relational construction, without assuming its output as an input witness.
After packing the computed blocker telescope, equality of executable
packages at any fixed sufficient budget implies exact equality of the
original annotated source terms. This injectivity statement does not
identify beta-convertible but syntactically different sources.
The implementation includes a reducible source
$\lambda x:o.(\lambda y:o.y)\,x$: its budget computes to $1$,
its closed quote computes to a normal term, and the closed decoder
recovers the original, still reducible source. A separate computation
checks subsequent normalization. These examples test the executable path;
the coverage and correctness theorems quantify over all source derivations.

The finite-telescope interface abstracts those blockers in descending order:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Code_{\leq d}(A)&=\Pi b_d:\Id_d.\cdots\Pi b_0:\Id_0.A,\\
\quoteof t_d&=\lambda b_d:\Id_d.\cdots\lambda b_0:\Id_0.q(t),\\
\Dec_{d,s}^{\mathrm{tel}}&=
\lambda A:\Ur s.\lambda q:\Code_{\leq d}(A).
q\,\id_d\cdots\id_0.
\end{aligned}
$$

For $A:\Ur s$, direct rank calculation gives
$\Code_{\leq d}(A):\Ur{\max(d+2,s)}$ and a decoder type
of rank $\max(d+3,s+1)$. `TelescopeDecoder.family_decoder_typing`
checks the actual decoder, with its exact rank expressed by the recursive
function `package_rank`. The theorem `fixed_decoder_bounded_quotation`
uses the same decoder for every source with budget $d$ and result order $s$.

Closing over the distinguished ground type also has an internal solution.
Let

$$
\begin{aligned}
K_s&=\Pi o:\Ur0.\Ur s,\\
C_d(P)&=\Pi o:\Ur0.\Code_{\leq d}(P\,o),\\
\Dec^{\mathrm{cl}}_{d,s}
 &=\lambda P:K_s.\lambda q:C_d(P).\lambda o:\Ur0.
     (q\,o)\,\id_d\cdots\id_0.
\end{aligned}
$$

This is one closed PTS term for each fixed pair $(d,s)$, with type
$\Pi P:K_s.C_d(P)\to(\Pi o:\Ur0.P\,o)$.
For a term-closed source $t:\sigma$ with its single free type variable
$o$, put $P_\sigma=\lambda o:\Ur0.|\sigma|$ and
$Q=\lambda o:\Ur0.\quoteof t_d$. The closed representation $Q$
is normal, and the actual decoder application satisfies

$$
\Dec^{\mathrm{cl}}_{d,s}\,P_\sigma\,Q
\bto\lambda o:\Ur0.t,
\qquad s=\operatorname{ord}(\sigma).
$$

`ClosedDecoder.closed_fixed_decoder_bounded_quotation` proves original
typing and strong recovery for this closed interface. The ground-type
abstraction is inside the decoder; it is not an external evaluation step.
Every independent STLC source derivation has a finite budget, so this
is an infinite fragment result. The fragment does not include all PTS-rk
syntax or the decoder's own polymorphic code, and no self-applicability
claim follows.

# Why shallow blocking does not solve dependent quotation

The rank-indexed identities solve a rank-selection problem. They do not
by themselves solve dependent conversion. Consider the well-formed context

$$
A:\Ur0,\quad B:\Pi x:A.\Ur0,\quad a:A,
\quad f:\Pi x:A.B\,x.
$$

The source term $f\,((\lambda x:A.x)\,a)$ can be given type $B\,a$.
If its inner redex is replaced by
$b_1\,(A\to A)\,(\lambda x:A.x)\,a$, that argument is neutral,
so its occurrence in the result type is no longer convertible to $a$.
Blocking other applications as well does not restore the lost conversion.
This example diagnoses failure of the naive, type-preserving shallow
translation; it is not a theorem that every dependent encoding is impossible.

The paper's Figure 2 term-reduction rules do not reduce inside type
annotations; PTS-rk's original reduction does. Its normality theorem
therefore cannot be imported as the stronger annotation-normality result
required here. One cannot avoid the dependent conversion issue by leaving
all type expressions untouched:
original PTS-rk reduction traverses lambda annotations and products, so
a reducible annotation would prevent full normality of the quote.
Nor may the typing theorem be replaced by a native Rocq checker judgment,
or supplied as a premise of the desired quotation theorem.

A deeper indexed construction needs jointly compatible translations
of contexts, types, terms, substitution, and conversion. An explicit
interpretation relation or transported representation of equality might
help, but the required witnesses must be built inside the original PTS.
The canonical-context, hereditary-substitution and distinct-index results
above supply this compatibility for metalevel original syntax. Lifting it
to a full object-language dependent representation is a separate step.
The Tarski operations above supply actual transport programs in a finite
normal signature, with typed identity instantiation, normal sort quotations,
variable-preserving dependent context extensions and normal local binders.
The smart fresh-variable operation solves its local annotation redex, not
by itself the global conversion induction or full quotation normality.
The checked equality-layer construction above supplies the missing repairs
and the full structural quotation theorem.
The extensional instantiation functions of Brown--Palsberg suggest a
direction; their correctness for $F_\omega$ does not supply this dependent
coherence theorem.
