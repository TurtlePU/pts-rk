A combinatory formulation of PTS-rk
October 23, 2026
Abstract
PTS-rk admits a complete Hilbert presentation by finitely many numerically indexed
axiom schemes, by the supersorted-system theorem of Bunder and Dekkers. A finite
basis of fixed closed typed combinators is nevertheless impossible: application cannot
increase the rank of its function’s type. We give rank-typed BCKW arithmetic, an
explicit Hilbert catalogue and a first-order algebraic presentation using quotient inductive-
inductive-recursive syntax. Checked soundness and extraction are distinguished from the
remaining formal completeness and adequacy proofs. All expansions retain the original
annotations and use beta conversion only. Elementary bounds control normalization and
every intermediate term; fixing the input’s universe labels gives finite syntax carriers and a
total, typed Agda apply-and-normalize function. Independent certificate checking gives
contextual soundness in graded finite carriers and external exclusion of a closed proof of
Void. Finite Church encodings give explicit rank-bounded code-to-code reduction programs,
separated from the proposed internal consistency proof. A ground-query Horn reduction
with explicit Void denials excludes contradictory models. Finite ground fragments have
exact, computable models; signed completion reflects all formulas in their finite atom
language. Quantified MM0 fragments have a complete fixed-domain constructor search
and proof-producing finite satisfaction certificates. The literal MM0 theory agrees with the
independent catalogue; all size/depth-guarded schemas have finite models with empty Void.
Each fixed finite fragment has an independent Church proof encoding and an internal
soundness function for arbitrary inhabitants, with proof-type rank at most three and
soundness-function type rank at most four. One finite, ungraded carrier cannot, however,
support all original PTS typings and conversion while keeping Void empty.
1 The calculus and its logical reading
Terms of PTS-rk are variables, universes Ui (i ∈ N), products Πx : A.B, annotated abstractions
λx : A.t, and applications tu. Contexts are lists of typed variables. Its distinctive rules are
Γ ⊢ A : Ua Γ, x : A ⊢ B : Ub
Γ ⊢ Ui : U0,
, r(a, b) = max(a + 1, b).
Γ ⊢ Πx : A.B : Ur(a,b)
Typing and conversion are those of the original calculus: annotations are retained, with neither
cumulativity nor eta conversion. In particular, Ui : U0 is not the usual ascending universe rule
Ui : Ui+1.
A logical judgment Γ ⊢ t : A reads: under assumptions Γ, t is a proof of the proposition
represented by A. Types may also describe data; there is no separate primitive universe of
propositions. A product Πx : A.B(x) is universal quantification over inputs x : A; when the
input and output are propositions and B does not depend on x, it is implication A → B.
Quantification over A : Ua allows types and propositions themselves as parameters.
Application is dependent elimination:
Γ ⊢ f : Πx : A.B(x) Γ ⊢ u : A
Γ ⊢ f u : B(u).

3 Why a fixed finite typed basis is impossible
Write ρ(t) = s when t : T and T : Us: this is the rank of the type of t. In a well-formed context
it is unique, invariant under beta conversion between typable terms, and satisfies
ρ(f u) ≤ ρ(f ).
Every universe atom has type rank zero, since Ui : U0 : U0. For finitely many fixed closed typed
atoms b1, . . . , bm, set R= max(0, ρ(b1), . . . , ρ(bm)). Induction on application trees bounds
every typable expansion by R, even when every Ui is available. The bound also holds for
typable beta-convertible outputs. But define
A0 = U0, v0 = U0, As+1 = Πx : As.U0,
vs+1 = λx : As.U0.
Then As : Us and vs : As, so vR+1 cannot be represented.
Theorem 3.1 (No finite fixed typed basis). No finite list of closed originally typed PTS-rk
terms is applicatively complete up to original beta conversion, even with all universe atoms
available. The representing application tree must itself be originally typable.
This excludes a fixed typed BCKW basis or a single fixed typed combinator, but not numerically
indexed families with unbounded type ranks. Such families supply the economical presentation
below.

5 A complete Hilbert presentation
Take the sorts {Ui : i ∈ N}, the axioms Ui : U0, and the four preceding schemes. Complete the
scheme set B by the following operations of Bunder–Dekkers, Definition 7.3 [1]. They operate
on schemes with sort parameters, before numerical instantiation.
1. Types of schemes. From M : T in B, with T not a sort, include T ′ : Us whenever the
original calculus types this scheme. Here T ′ is T , or is obtained by replacing a repeated
occurrence of a sort parameter in T by a fresh sort parameter.
2. Expose a product. Whenever B contains
(λx1 : A1· · · λxk−1 : Ak−1.Πxk : Ak.B) : (Πx1 : A1· · · Πxk−1 : Ak−1.Us),
also include the corresponding well-typed scheme
(λx1 : A1· · · λxk : Ak.B) : (Πx1 : A1· · · Πxk : Ak.Ut).
Only the sort constraints needed for each new scheme’s own typing are retained. They
are constraints on fixed scheme shapes, not assumptions that the theorem being translated
is already derivable. The resulting catalogue has 11 product, 7 identity, 17 constant and
30 substitution shapes [1, Section 7]. Specialize their sort constraints using Ui : U0 and
r(a, b) = max(a + 1, b). Thus one family indexed by a tag from 65 shapes and a finite tuple

PTS-rk is supersorted: every constant has a sort and every pair of sorts has a product

⊢H M : A ⇐⇒ ⊢rk M : A .
This is an external completeness theorem, not a Rocq theorem or axiom of this development.
It supplies a Hilbert logical system: closed proofs of exactly the original closed judgments arise
from fixed schematic logical axioms and the four rules above. Only its annotated, beta-only
presentation is used here.
Theorem 5.1 (Hilbert soundness and proof extraction). For any catalogue whose original
atom typings have been independently proved, a Hilbert derivation of M : A yields a binder-free
applicative code c such that
⊢rk M : A, ⊢rk E(c) : A, E(c) −→∗
β M.
Here E expands catalogue atoms and preserves application.
The atom-typing premise belongs to the metatheorem, not the definition of a Hilbert derivation.
Extraction retains a code through both reduction rules; its expansion recovers the subject by
forward beta reduction, not merely conversion. For the literal 65-shape catalogue below, atom
typing is discharged: soundness and extraction hold without an additional premise. The
extracted code represents a proof through the original typing of E(c); types still use original
annotated syntax.

Corollary 5.2 (Completeness of the literal catalogue). The 65-shape Hilbert system derives
exactly the closed judgments of original PTS-rk. This mathematical result uses [1]; its
completeness direction is not yet a Rocq theorem.
To prove it, first retain all 66 templates. A declared type is a universe, a core type, or a
pure-product prefix ending in a universe. Freshening a repeated sort occurrence in operation
(1) therefore only renames an independent domain slot or frees the final slot; the table
contains every well-typed instance. Operation (2) increments e, and all exposures are present.
The original functional sort rules fix each declared type. Thus the 66 templates contain
the schematic catalogue of Definition 7.3; Theorem 9.4 supplies completeness by inclusion.
Soundness supplies the reverse implication, and the checked 65/66 equivalence removes K13.
The symbolic template-inclusion step is not formalized in Rocq.
The formalized support includes contextual start and empty-context equivalence, internal
substitution, classification of the seven variable/application-ended heads, literal product-
exposure compatibility and beta simulation, I, K, S abstraction and product assembly given
the required family derivations, and the local type-preserving six-to-three S contraction
with shared premises. General chain shortening with a sharing-aware measure, internal
type correctness and unconditional deduction admissibility remain unformalized. The badges
certify the supporting results, not the completeness direction.
5.2 A Metamath Zero specification
This Hilbert calculus has a first-order specification in Metamath Zero (MM0) [6]. The
accompanying CombinatoryPTS-rk.mm0 uses strict sorts for natural-number indices, raw de
Bruijn terms, finite ranked telescopes and provable statements. Lambda annotations remain
part of the term syntax; beta reduction retains capture-avoiding substitution. Auxiliary Horn
rules compute ranks, shifts, substitutions and reductions. The graph form e L D E M T folds a

ranked telescope L over a body/type pair D:E, exposing e initial lambdas and leaving products
afterwards. Only fixed axiom rows feed Hilbert typing: they generate the 11 product, 7 identity,
17 constant and 30 substitution shapes, without an opaque catalogue lookup or original-typing
oracle.
Its Hilbert inference layer is the following literal source excerpt:
term h: tm > tm > judgment;
axiom h_universe (i: nat): $ h (u i) (u z) $;
axiom h_scheme (M A: tm): $ ax M A $ > $ h M A $;
axiom h_apply (f x A B T: tm):
$ h f (pi A B) $ > $ h x A $ > $ sub z x B T $ > $ h (app f x) T $;
axiom h_convert (M A B C: tm) (r: nat):
$ h M A $ > $ h B (u r) $ > $ red A C $ > $ red B C $ > $ h M B $;
axiom h_subject (M N A: tm): $ h M A $ > $ red M N $ > $ h N A $;
axiom h_type (M A B: tm): $ h M A $ > $ red A B $ > $ h M B $;
Here an MM0 axiom declares a rule of this specified logical system; the arrow between dollar-
delimited statements separates premises from conclusion. It is not a new assumption in the
Rocq development. Read the rules by their least syntactic derivability relation, not as a
claim that every first-order model has only the intended numbers or terms. Only the fixed
closed templates and universe terms initialize Hilbert typing; application, conversion and
reduction then preserve closedness. In h_convert, C is a common beta reduct of the two types;
original confluence identifies this condition with beta conversion. An MM0 proof certifies such
a derivation, while the PTS subject is the proof object it types. The specification and ten
example derivations1, including capture-avoiding substitution, pass the MM0 elaborator and
reference checker. These checks do not establish the general Rocq correspondence or source
completeness. The full source is included in Appendix A, directly from the same file; it is
longer than one page.

6.1 Operations and equations
The context operations are empty context and extension Γ ◃ A. Substitutions have identity,
composition, the unique substitution to the empty context, guarded pairing, and projections.
Write their actions as A[σ] and t[σ]. In addition to the category laws, impose
A[id] = A, A[σ][ν] = A[σ ◦ ν],
t[id] = t, t[σ][ν] = t[σ ◦ ν],
fst(σ, t) = σ, snd(σ, t) = t, (fst σ, snd σ) = σ,
(σ, t)◦ ν = (σ ◦ ν, t[ν]).

lifting a substitution through a binder uses the type-composition path. These structural guards

7 Elementary normalization and finite syntax carriers
7.1 The rank parameter and the reduction strategy
Let |t| count all constructors of the original annotated syntax, including annotations. Use
Knuth’s tetration notation a ↑↑ 0 = 1 and a ↑↑ (h + 1) = aa↑↑h, and put q= max(2, N ). The
relevant bound d bounds the type rank of every redex head in t, also inside annotations. It
is the repository’s RedexBound, not merely the outer type rank ρ(t). Indeed the closed terms
ta = (λx : (ΠA : Ua.A → A).U0)Ia
all have type U0 and 13 nodes, while their root redex has rank a + 2. This separates the two
rank notions; it is not a lower bound on the cost of reducing these particular terms.
Theorem 7.1 (Normal form and intermediate-size bound). Suppose Γ is well formed, Γ ⊢ t : A,
|t|= N , and RedexBound holds at d. There is a deterministic beta-reduction sequence to a
normal t∗ with Γ ⊢ t∗ : A. Every intermediate term, including t∗, has at most q ↑↑ (d + 1)
nodes and retains redex-rank bound d. The documented abstract normalization cost is at most
(N + 5d + 2) ↑↑ (2d + 2).
Use d passes of the existing greedy normalizer [8]. Within a pass, reduce the annotation or
left child first, then the body or right child, then contract the root when possible. A pass
lowers the maximal redex rank; sequentialization bounds every single-beta intermediate by
2|t|. Iterating this binary exponential d times gives a bound no larger than q ↑↑ (d + 1); the
comparison is checked. Original subject reduction and redex-rank preservation supply the
typing and rank invariants. For fixed d, these functions are elementary, with tower heights
linear in d.
For any fixed finite set of atom shapes, including the indexed catalogue, expansion satisfies
|E(c)| ≤ Cℓ when c has ℓ atoms, with a shape-dependent constant C. Thus max(2, Cℓ) ↑↑ (d+1)
bounds normalization of its expansion. For type-indexed atoms the bound requires fixed type
shapes, as in the numeral family; arbitrary type-index payloads must also be charged. This does
not yet bound the length of a normal binder-free BCKW output: an annotation-preserving
compiler back to that syntax, with a size theorem, is still needed. Numerical labels count as
one node here; their bit lengths must be charged in a machine-level complexity claim.
7.2 Finite normal families and dependent application
Rank and length alone do not make normal forms finite: the infinitely many distinct Ui : U0
are all closed normal terms of size one and type rank zero. Fix instead a finite alphabet Σ of
universe labels. For a fixed type A, the desired family of syntax codes is
NFΣ(ℓ, r; A) = {t | labels(t) ⊆ Σ, |t|= ℓ, t normal, ⊢ t : A, ⊢ A : Ur}.

(4N + 4) ↑↑ (d + 3), Σ = labels(t).

Running r passes on f y returns a normal z : B[y/x]. Every intermediate has size at most

The last rank bound needs no internal rank inference: normal f, y have no internal redexes, so
only the new root redex matters. Its rank is r. The algorithm can represent each term state
by an index into the displayed finite list; the proofs check the underlying normalizer and its
finite envelope, not an extracted finite-memory machine. They do not bound the storage of
arbitrary typing witnesses. Nor is an externally supplied, possibly beta-expanded B bounded
by |f | + |y|: count supplied type syntax and its rank budget separately if types themselves are
to be normalized.

7.7 Fragment interpretations and internal consistency
The result above is all-six-rule contextual soundness in a graded syntactic interpretation. Every
fixed typed input has computed finite coverage, and larger grades cover more inputs. These
graded results alone do not make each stage a finite first-order model of a logical fragment.
That requires a total interpretation of the chosen signature on fixed finite domains, coherent
dependent fibres, and all chosen axioms under every semantic valuation. Operations that
increase grades do not automatically supply this closure. The explicitly guarded construction
below supplies fixed finite domains; its axioms retain their bounds rather than asserting
unrestricted closure. For example, iterating A0 = U0 and Ak+1 = Πx : U0.Ak gives normal
types of rank one for k > 0, using only label zero and 2k + 1 nodes: a fixed size-bounded
faithful type carrier cannot contain the whole family. This does not rule out nonfaithful finite
models.
There is a sharper obstruction if raw computation is interpreted as equality in a total, untyped
applicative algebra. On a finite carrier, the equation (Kx)y= x makes x → Kx injective,
hence surjective. Choose a with Ka= K. Then Kx = (Ka)x = a for every x, so injectivity
makes the carrier a singleton. Any positive typing fact then contradicts an empty Void fibre.
Neither extensionality nor another combinator is needed. This theorem does not apply
to typed or resource-indexed application, or to MM0’s directed red predicate, which is not
equality. Finite models of reduction and partial interpretations are a possible alternative [11];
their dependent typing and conversion soundness for PTS-rk must still be established. A
finite model of selected ground instances, constructed below, does not assert closure under all
quantified instances.

7.8 A locally finitely satisfiable target
There is a precise ground-query reduction, distinct from a faithful finite model of syntax.
Ground the Hilbert inference rules, including the auxiliary syntax, substitution and reduction
graphs. Introduce one nullary predicate pJ for each closed judgment J , and, for every ground
rule instance, add
J1, . . . , Jk ⇒ J → (pJ1 ∧ · · · ∧ pJk ) ⇒ pJ.
Purely positive clauses allow the valuation making every atom true, including every alleged
proof of Void. To exclude it, add the denials
p t:Void ⇒ ⊥ for every closed subject t.
More generally a denial forbids simultaneous truth of a finite list of atoms. A constrained
model satisfies the positive clauses and makes every denial’s premises false jointly. Thus every
model, not merely the chosen least one, has an empty ground Void fibre. This is an effective
Horn theory with constraints in a countably infinite recursive vocabulary; finite conversion
certificates can be enumerated without deciding raw conversion.
Theorem 7.4 (Consistency-sensitive ground reduction). The constrained theory has a model
exactly when no forbidden list is jointly derivable. Under this consistency condition, J is
derivable exactly when every constrained model satisfies pJ . The least model interprets pJ as
derivability and reflects every atom. Every finite ground subtheory has a one-element-domain
constrained model.
Soundness is induction on derivations; reflection uses the least model. Its satisfaction of the
denials is the consistency obligation, not an automatic property of Horn clauses. Restricting the
rules preserves this obligation. Conversely, every derivation uses finitely many rule instances,
so satisfiability of all finite ground subtheories implies that no forbidden derivation exists.
The singleton domain carries no syntax: independent nullary predicates retain the judgment
distinctions. A singleton domain does not mean that these predicates are all true.

9 Future work
The following three directions concern proposed systems, not changes to the original calculus
or results established above.
1. A quadratically checkable PTS-rk variant. Apply the ideas of van den Berg
and den Besten [10]: replace implicit definitional conversion by explicit propositional
computation and transport witnesses. Develop an objective variant preserving the rank
discipline, and prove deterministic worst-case O(L2) type checking, where L includes the
context, term, type, numerical ranks and all annotations and witnesses. Their Theorem
7 proves quadratic checking for objective type theory; adapting it to PTS-rk is a separate
obligation. This bounds checking, not proof search or normalization.
26
2. A logic-free arithmetic of partial functions. Develop a system analogous to
logic-free primitive recursive arithmetic, with the following requirements.
• Terms denote partial functions, rather than necessarily total functions. The basic
judgment is extension, not equality:
f ⊑ g ⇐⇒ ∀⃗x, y f (⃗x) ↓ y ⇒ g(⃗x) ↓ y .
Thus the graph of f is included in the graph of g; symmetry is not an inference
rule.
• Every partial computable function must be expressible using a finite term-forming
basis, in the spirit of finite bases for elementary recursive arithmetic. Computational
completeness is a requirement on the intended standard interpretation, not on each
finite model.
• Use reflexivity, transitivity and substitution, together with a suitable induction rule.
Substitution and induction must be sound for partiality and extension. Require a
finite core of axioms and one infinite scheme existsn, whose intended meaning is:
either all numbers are equal, or there is an initial segment of at least n distinct
natural numbers. This is a semantic specification to encode in the extension calculus,
not an addition of unrestricted first-order logic.
• Require local finite satisfiability: for every finite numeral k, the finite core together
with all existsn for n ≤ k has a finite model validating the inference rules. Noncol-
lapsed finite models realizing the retained initial segments are a stronger, useful
goal; the collapse disjunct must not be mistaken for such a construction.
• Require deterministic worst-case O(L2) derivation checking, or better, in the full
encoded derivation length, including numerals, substitutions and induction cer-
tificates. Checking must not decide semantic extension of arbitrary programs or
execute potentially divergent computations.
3. Interpretation of the type theory in the arithmetic. Construct an effective,
derivation-preserving interpretation of the variant in (1) inside the system in (2). Spec-
ify the translations of typing, explicit computation witnesses and contradiction, with
sufficient reflection or model soundness to support the intended consistency application.
This must go beyond constrained ground-query reduction: retain contradiction exclusion
while interpreting the quantified signature and justifying the required internal reflection.
Positive finite satisfiability alone does not exclude the translated contradiction.
